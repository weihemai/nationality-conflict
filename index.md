# A Guide for Parents of Children with Chinese and Other Nationalities
## Navigating Nationality Conflicts, Identity Documents, and Travel

> **About this document.** This guide was written with AI assistance, sourced from Chinese government materials and supplemented with real-life case experience from a community of 400+ families in China. It has been carefully reviewed to describe the situation as it actually stands, but no guarantee of correctness can be given, and rules, regulations and their interpretation may change at any time. Verify anything you plan to rely on with the authority that will handle your case.

> **About the author.** I'm a German citizen, first came to China as language student in 1998, have now been living for over 20 years in Shanghai, and am married almost as long to a Jiangsu native. Both our children were born here: our son, now at university in Germany, and our almost teenage daughter, at the German school in Shanghai. Both hold German passports and Chinese Travel Documents; our son has hukou in Jiangsu and a mainland ID card, and our daughter's registration is under way. Years of dealing with Chinese and foreign authorities on dual nationality — or: nationality conflict, as China prefers to call it — have taught us a great deal. Our aim has always been to keep both options open until the children can decide for themselves. It has worked so far, with hiccups and administrative detours along the way. Being reasonably fluent in Mandarin has let me take on conversations my spouse was sometimes hesitant to have, and a firm grasp of the concepts, actual laws and regulations has proved essential: officials are not always well-versed in their own legal system, and decisions can turn on gut feeling, the mood of the day, hearsay, half-knowledge, or simple fear of the consequences. Logic and persistence have always got us there.

# Introduction

This guide is designed for parents of children who hold—or may hold—both Chinese nationality and one or more other nationalities. The term **国籍冲突 (nationality conflict)** refers to a situation where a child is a Chinese national under Chinese law but also holds a foreign nationality under the law of another country. The term does not appear in the Nationality Law itself [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html); it is used in Ministry of Public Security (MPS) rules and consular practice [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html)[\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm).

Because the **《中华人民共和国国籍法》 (Nationality Law of the People's Republic of China)** does not recognize dual nationality for Chinese citizens [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html), families face unique challenges when it comes to birth registration, identity documents, and international travel. This guide walks you through the process from birth to adulthood. Many procedures are set by individual cities or consulates; where a rule comes from one city or one consulate, the guide says so.

**What this guide does not cover.** It deals only with the Chinese side of a nationality conflict. It does not, and cannot, address what the other country of nationality requires — passport rules, registration of births abroad, military service, tax, or how that country treats a child who also holds Chinese nationality. Those rules differ by country and change over time, and the two sides have to be worked out together. For that half of the picture, contact your embassy or consulate in China or the authorities at home, take legal advice where the stakes warrant it, and compare notes with other families in the same position.

## 1.2 The Core Legal Principle

> *"中华人民共和国不承认中国公民具有双重国籍。"* (Nationality Law Art. 3: The People's Republic of China does not recognize dual nationality for any Chinese citizen.) [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html)

When a person has both Chinese nationality and a foreign nationality, **China recognizes only the Chinese nationality**. The National Immigration Administration (NIA) has confirmed that China does not recognize the foreign nationality of a person who is Chinese under the law [\[S12\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=XnD%2Fs0j%2FZ6YV1zBzlKiEEg%3D%3D). Chinese embassies and consulates therefore **do not issue a Chinese visa** in such a person's foreign passport; they issue a **Travel Document** instead [\[S33\]](http://wqb.hunan.gov.cn/xxgk/lbxx/201903/t20190320_5298474.html). Parents cannot choose between a visa and a Travel Document: the consulate decides, based on the place of birth, the child's foreign nationality and the parents' residence status at the time of birth [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm).

## 1.3 How Nationality Is Determined Under Chinese Law

The **《中华人民共和国国籍法》 (Nationality Law of the PRC)** sets out the following rules [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html):

- **Article 3**: The PRC does not recognize dual nationality for Chinese citizens.
- **Article 4**: A person born in China with at least one Chinese-national parent has Chinese nationality.
- **Article 5**: A person born abroad with at least one Chinese-national parent has Chinese nationality—**except** where the child acquired a foreign nationality at birth **and** the Chinese-national parent(s) had **settled abroad**.
- **Article 9**: Chinese citizens who **settle abroad** and **voluntarily acquire foreign nationality** automatically lose Chinese nationality.

**When both parents are Chinese, one parent's settlement is enough.** The 2008 MPS/MFA notice on Article 5 (公境〔2008〕2204号) is transmitted in full by the Zhejiang Public Security Department, which publishes it in its register of normative documents, dated 24 September 2008 and marked **still in force** [\[S6\]](https://gat.zj.gov.cn/art/2015/1/9/art_1229098253_600299.html). Its operative passage reads:

> *"根据《国籍法》第五条规定，本人出生在外国，其父母均为中国公民，或父母一方为中国公民的，本人具有中国国籍。但是本人在外国出生时即具有外国国籍，并具有下列情形之一的，不具有中国国籍：一、父母双方为中国公民并均定居在外国；二、父母一方为外国人，另一方为中国公民并定居在外国；三、父母双方为中国公民，其中一方定居在外国。"* [\[S6\]](https://gat.zj.gov.cn/art/2015/1/9/art_1229098253_600299.html)

**Translation**: A person born abroad to two Chinese parents, or to one Chinese parent, has Chinese nationality. But a person who held a foreign nationality at birth does **not** have Chinese nationality in any of these three cases: (1) both parents are Chinese and **both** have settled abroad; (2) one parent is foreign and the Chinese parent has settled abroad; (3) **both parents are Chinese and one of them has settled abroad**.

The third case is the one families most often miss: where both parents are Chinese, the permanent residency of **either** one is enough to leave the child without Chinese nationality. The same outcome appears in the UK Embassy's reference table and the Los Angeles Consulate's eligibility rules [\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm)[\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).

**If the child has no Chinese nationality, naturalization is possible.** The same notice adds that children of overseas Chinese who acquired a foreign nationality at birth may apply to **acquire** Chinese nationality under Article 7 of the Nationality Law [\[S6\]](https://gat.zj.gov.cn/art/2015/1/9/art_1229098253_600299.html). Article 7 admits applicants who are close relatives of Chinese nationals, have settled in China, or have other legitimate reasons [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html). **This is not a route to dual nationality**: Article 8 provides that a person approved for Chinese nationality may not keep their foreign nationality [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html).

The MFA stresses that nationality cannot be inferred from the parents' nationality alone: their settlement status and the place of birth also count, so children in the same family can end up with different nationalities [\[S18\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/gyls/lsgz/mtwz/rmrbhwblsfwzl_645631/201306/t20130626_932301.shtml).

# Part Two: Birth and Early Years

## 2.1 Before Birth: What to Consider

If you are a Chinese citizen living abroad or in a cross-national marriage (跨国婚姻), consider:

1. **Where will the child be born?** The place of birth affects whether the child acquires a foreign nationality at birth.
2. **Does either Chinese parent hold foreign permanent residency?** If either Chinese parent holds it at the time of birth and the child acquires a foreign nationality at birth, the child generally does **not** have Chinese nationality [\[S6\]](https://gat.zj.gov.cn/art/2015/1/9/art_1229098253_600299.html)[\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm).
3. **What is the Chinese parents' legal status in the country of birth?** Consulates ask for the residence permits or visas the parents held at the birth. The Jamaica Embassy says it cannot issue a Travel Document if the Chinese parents have no legal status there or their status cannot be established [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)[\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).
4. **Where will the child primarily reside?** This affects which travel documents will be needed.

## 2.2 At Birth: Nationality Determination

**For children born abroad:**
> *"父母双方或一方为中国公民，本人出生在外国，具有中国国籍；但父母双方或一方为中国公民并定居在外国，本人出生时即具有外国国籍的，不具有中国国籍。"* (Nationality Law Art. 5) [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html)

**Translation**: A child born abroad to one or two Chinese-national parents has Chinese nationality—**unless** the child had a foreign nationality at birth **and** the Chinese-national parent(s) had settled abroad (in consular practice: held foreign permanent residency) at the time of birth. If both parents are Chinese, it is enough that **one** of them had settled abroad [\[S6\]](https://gat.zj.gov.cn/art/2015/1/9/art_1229098253_600299.html)[\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm).

**For children born in China to one foreign parent:** a child born in China to one Chinese and one foreign parent, who acquires the foreign parent's nationality at birth under that country's law, has Chinese nationality under Article 4 **and** a foreign nationality — a **nationality conflict**. This is the third category in the Los Angeles Consulate's list of people eligible for a Travel Document [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm). (An earlier version of this guide quoted a Chinese sentence for this category whose source could not be traced; it has been replaced by the consulate's own wording.)

## 2.3 The Birth Certificate: Name Requirements

### If the child is born in China

- The **《出生医学证明》 (Medical Certificate of Birth)** must be completed in **standard Chinese characters** (规范汉字), and once issued its contents are in principle not changed [\[S76\]](https://www.shhuangpu.gov.cn/zw/009002/009002016/009002016002/009002016002001/20150713/f0a0214b-dcb2-4252-99f9-2264f6bd8ab9.html).
- **If one or both parents are foreign, the name field may be completed in Chinese or English.** Shanghai puts it plainly: *"新生儿父母一方或双方为外籍的，'新生儿姓名'栏可填写中文或英文"* (Art. 13(三)1). The parents' own names may likewise be in Chinese or English, while their other details are in Chinese (Art. 13(四)1) [\[S76\]](https://www.shhuangpu.gov.cn/zw/009002/009002016/009002016002/009002016002001/20150713/f0a0214b-dcb2-4252-99f9-2264f6bd8ab9.html).

**Changing an English name to a Chinese one so that *hukou* can be registered.** This is one of the most frequent problems for these families: the certificate carries an English name, but *hukou* registration needs standard Chinese characters. The route is a **reissue (换发)** of the birth certificate, and the key document comes from the police:

- **Shanghai** provides for exactly this case. Article 16(一) allows a reissue *"因户口登记机关提供相关证明不能进行出生登记而需变更新生儿姓名的"* — where the *hukou* registration office has certified that birth registration cannot proceed and the newborn's name therefore has to be changed. The reissue is made *"应凭户口登记机关出具的《〈出生医学证明〉信息变更单》"* — **on production of the 《〈出生医学证明〉信息变更单》 (Birth Certificate Information Change Form, Annex 7), completed and stamped by the hukou registration office**, i.e. the police station where the child will be registered [\[S76\]](https://www.shhuangpu.gov.cn/zw/009002/009002016/009002016002/009002016002001/20150713/f0a0214b-dcb2-4252-99f9-2264f6bd8ab9.html).
- The parents do not fill this form in themselves: it is **issued and stamped by the police station**, so ask for it at the *hukou* counter that is refusing the registration. No public download of the blank form could be found on a Shanghai government site; what the counter hands over is the completed, stamped original, which is what the hospital needs.
- The application then goes back to the **issuing hospital** (原签发机构) with the 《〈出生医学证明〉换发申请登记表》 (Annex 8). Shanghai gives the hospital **7 working days** to reissue where the papers are complete (Art. 17) [\[S76\]](https://www.shhuangpu.gov.cn/zw/009002/009002016/009002016002/009002016002001/20150713/f0a0214b-dcb2-4252-99f9-2264f6bd8ab9.html).
- **Before any *hukou* registration has been made**, the original certificate's 正页 and 副页 are both handed in and both are replaced, and the old certificate is voided (Art. 17(二)). **If the child is already registered**, only the 正页 is replaced, and a copy of the original 副页 stamped by the police station with the 户口专用章 must be supplied (Art. 17(一)) [\[S76\]](https://www.shhuangpu.gov.cn/zw/009002/009002016/009002016002/009002016002001/20150713/f0a0214b-dcb2-4252-99f9-2264f6bd8ab9.html).
- A reissue corrects **only** the item that was wrong; everything else must match the original certificate (Art. 16) [\[S76\]](https://www.shhuangpu.gov.cn/zw/009002/009002016/009002016002/009002016002001/20150713/f0a0214b-dcb2-4252-99f9-2264f6bd8ab9.html).
- **Other provinces work the same way but name the paperwork differently.** Guangdong allows the reissue when the public security bureau issues a letter stating that birth registration cannot proceed, including where the name uses non-standard characters [\[S43\]](https://wsjkw.gd.gov.cn/zwyw/content/mpost_4331755.html). Check the local health commission's rules before choosing an English-only name if you may register *hukou* later.
- Shanghai's own policy interpretations summarise the same rules [\[S48\]](https://www.pudong.gov.cn/019023004/20220102/410880.html).

### If the child is born abroad

- Travel Document applications record both the child's Chinese and foreign-language names (e.g., the Los Angeles declaration asks for both) [\[S24\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/cybg/hzlxzbg/202506/P020250625227680798412.pdf).
- A Chinese **passport** holder can apply for an endorsement (加注) of a foreign-language name, a former name or a traditional-character name (MPS measures Art. 9) [\[S4\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1295795/content.html). Calgary confirms that a name endorsement can be added to a passport **or a Travel Document** where there is a proper reason, on production of a birth certificate, marriage certificate or residence permit that is legally valid locally; for a minor both parents must consent, in person or by notarized written consent [\[S72\]](https://calgary.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlx/202506/t20250619_11652989.htm). Travel Documents also have an observation page (备注页) for endorsements [\[S72\]](https://calgary.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlx/202506/t20250619_11652989.htm); ask the consulate whether a foreign-name endorsement is available.
**The name on the Travel Document is not the same thing as a name in the *hukou*.**

- A Travel Document for a nationality-conflict child normally shows **both**: the Chinese name in characters, and the foreign name exactly as it appears in the foreign passport. The Los Angeles declaration asks for both names for this reason [\[S24\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/cybg/hzlxzbg/202506/P020250625227680798412.pdf).
- **[Practice report]** Where the birth certificate carries no Chinese name, the consulate asks the parents to supply one on the application form, and that is the name printed on the Travel Document.
- **[Practice report] That name will usually not carry over to *hukou* registration** unless it also appears on the **Chinese birth certificate**. A name that exists only on the Travel Document is, from the registration office's point of view, a name no Chinese record supports. Some certificates do carry both — the older bilingual versions, and the practice still followed by some hospitals in Guangzhou — and those are accepted. A few families have nonetheless registered *hukou* where the certificate showed only an English name, so offices are not uniform.
- **The reliable fix is to get the Chinese name onto the birth certificate**, by the reissue route above, rather than relying on the Travel Document to supply it.

- For *hukou* registration in China, the foreign birth certificate needs a Chinese translation by a qualified translation agency **and** authentication: consular legalization, an **apostille** (for Apostille Convention states, since 7 November 2023), or a notarial certificate, depending on local rules [\[S19\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/gyls/lsgz/fwxx/202310/t20231023_11166241.shtml)[\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm)[\[S37\]](http://gaj.dg.gov.cn/gb2021/ztzl/crjgl/crjxx/content/post_4350555.html). See 7.4.

### Name Change After Registration

- **Legal basis**: under the Hukou Registration Regulations (1958) Art. 18, a person under 18 changes a name through themselves or their parents/adoptive parents; a person aged 18 or over applies personally [\[S8\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2531969.html).
- **Local conditions apply.** Example: Zhejiang requires both parents to agree to a minor's name change and, for a child aged 10 or older, the child's own consent [\[S45\]](https://www.kecheng.gov.cn/art/2022/4/1/art_1229449831_59037843.html). Other localities may set different conditions.

# Part Three: Household Registration (Hukou)

## 3.1 What Is Hukou and Why Does It Matter?

**户口 (*Hukou*)** is the official family register in China. It records a person's identity, place of residence, and family relationships. Having *hukou* is important for access to education, healthcare, and many other services in China.

Children with nationality conflicts are Chinese citizens and receive national treatment in China, but local *hukou* policies differ, so requirements vary by city [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm).

**Important — what *hukou* does and does not mean for nationality:**

- *Hukou* is a record of where a person is registered as living in China. It is not a nationality decision and not a travel document.
- Registering *hukou* does **not** end the nationality conflict. The other country still treats the person as its national under its own law, China still does not recognize that nationality, and the person still needs Chinese documents to enter and leave China (Parts Four and Five).
- Having *hukou* is consistent with holding Chinese nationality — registration presupposes it, and the public security bureau may require a nationality determination before registering (see 3.3) [\[S39\]](http://gdga.gd.gov.cn/bsfw/zsk/qt/content/post_3368370.html)[\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm).
- **Not** having *hukou* does not mean the person lacks Chinese nationality. Nationality-conflict children living abroad usually have no *hukou* at all. Asked directly whether a New Zealand-born child who returned on a Travel Document is Chinese despite having neither *hukou* nor a Resident ID Card, the NIA answered by quoting Article 5 alone — that is, the question turns on birth, parentage and settlement, not on registration [\[S68\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/preShowQuestion.jsp?MZ=1TIylb4HoPcp7vWV49%2f6Lw%3d%3d).
- Chinese nationality is lost only under Art. 9 or Art. 11 of the Nationality Law (see 6.1) [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html). That is a separate matter from *hukou*, though someone who has lost Chinese nationality must then apply to cancel their *hukou* and hand back the Resident ID Card (Shanghai rules) [\[S46\]](https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/gwk/search/content/GKXX-20230426101209846--2642).

## 3.2 Registering Hukou for a Child Born Abroad

Rules are set locally. The examples below are from Tianjin and Fuzhou; check the rules of the city where you will register.

**Tianjin example** [\[S40\]](https://ga.tj.gov.cn/bmfw/bszn/renkou/202202/t20220227_5815023.html):

- **Eligibility**: The child has Chinese nationality, at least one parent is a Chinese citizen with Tianjin *hukou*, and the child entered China with a Chinese passport or Travel Document.
- **Who applies**: The father or mother in person (no proxies), at the police station where the parent's *hukou* is registered.
- **Materials**: 《国外出生户口登记承诺书》 (registration undertaking); the foreign birth certificate; the passport or Travel Document that the child and the Chinese parent(s) used for their last entry; the parents' marriage certificate; the Chinese parent(s)' Resident ID Card; the 《居民户口簿》 (Household Register) of the household the child joins.
- **Under 6**: Community police check; approved by the head of the police station.
- **Aged 6 to under 18**:

> *"年满6周岁不满18周岁的，还应当提交亲子鉴定证明，逐级报公安分局审核后办理。"*

**Translation**: For children aged 6 to under 18, a **亲子鉴定证明 (parentage/DNA test certificate)** must also be submitted, and the application is reviewed level by level up to the district Public Security Sub-bureau.

- **Doubtful nationality**: The municipal exit-entry corps makes a nationality determination.

**Fuzhou example** [\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm) — the child is registered under the rules for persons who are not overseas Chinese (非华侨). Materials include:

- a 《国籍认定意见》 (nationality determination opinion), where required (see 3.3);
- the parents' travel documents used to return to China, their Household Register(s), Resident ID Cards and marriage certificate;
- the foreign birth certificate with a translation by a qualified agency, plus consular legalization, a treaty-recognized certificate (apostille) or a notarial certificate;
- **a declaration or proof that the child has not acquired, or has given up, foreign citizenship or foreign permanent residency**.

**[Practice report] Declarations and "commitment letters".** Fuzhou's published list asks for a declaration about foreign citizenship, and families report that some offices go further and press for a signed undertaking to **give up** the other nationality. Parents who meet this have three options, and the choice is theirs:

- **Ask what the requirement rests on.** Registration follows from the child having been found to be Chinese: the published procedures turn on the nationality determination plus the family's own documents [\[S39\]](http://gdga.gd.gov.cn/bsfw/zsk/qt/content/post_3368370.html)[\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm)[\[S74\]](https://english.pudong.gov.cn/2017-07/17/c_84199.htm). An undertaking about the other nationality is a local addition, not something the national framework asks for, and it is fair to ask the office where it comes from.
- **Negotiate it down** to a factual statement of the position (the child holds X nationality; the parents understand China does not recognise it) rather than a promise about the future.
- **Sign it.** The common view among families is that such an undertaking has no legal effect, for two reasons. Whether the child keeps or loses the other nationality is decided by **that country's** law, which a Chinese form cannot alter — the NIA says the same thing from the other direction, that giving up a foreign nationality is not something it administers and the way back to Chinese nationality is restoration under Article 13 [\[S85\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=9PQAMhZWr6Zk9fibcZHJMA%3D%3D). And the registration itself rests on the child **already being a Chinese national**, established by the nationality determination [\[S39\]](http://gdga.gd.gov.cn/bsfw/zsk/qt/content/post_3368370.html)[\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm) — so a *hukou* lawfully registered on that basis is not contingent on the undertaking being honoured, and not following through is not a listed ground for cancelling it.

This is the families' reading, not an official statement; no authority has been asked to confirm it. If an office insists in writing, keep a copy of what you signed.

If the child leaves China before the registration is approved, the application must be filed again [\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm).

**Shanghai example** [\[S46\]](https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/gwk/search/content/GKXX-20230426101209846--2642)[\[S47\]](https://zwdt.sh.gov.cn/govPortals/bsfw/item/086a73b2-d5f6-4dff-b33a-cc8f1bbab381):

- **Born in China**: registration uses the 《出生医学证明》. A parentage (DNA) report is **not** required as a rule; Shanghai lists the cases in which it is, e.g., no valid birth certificate, parents' details that differ from the certificate, birth outside a midwifery institution, a foreign birth certificate not legalized by the Chinese embassy/consulate, doubtful records, or implausible birth circumstances. There is no age-based DNA requirement like Tianjin's.
- **Born abroad**: a child **under 16** can be registered at the police station of the parent's Shanghai *hukou* if the child **has not acquired, or has given up, foreign nationality** and entered China with a Chinese passport, Travel Document or Exit-Entry Permit; from age 16 the rules for overseas Chinese settling in Shanghai apply (Shanghai Hukou Rules Art. 22) [\[S46\]](https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/gwk/search/content/GKXX-20230426101209846--2642).
- **Foreign documents**: legalized by the Chinese embassy/consulate or notarized; from Apostille Convention states, an apostille; non-Chinese documents need a translation by a qualified Shanghai translation agency [\[S47\]](https://zwdt.sh.gov.cn/govPortals/bsfw/item/086a73b2-d5f6-4dff-b33a-cc8f1bbab381).

**Which 民族 (ethnic group) is registered.** Parents often expect "其他 / Other" for a child of a mixed marriage. That is no longer how it is done. The 《中国公民民族成份登记管理办法》 (SEAC and MPS Order 2015 No. 2, in force 1 January 2016) governs the entry [\[S75\]](https://www.neac.gov.cn/seac/c103280/202111/1154721.shtml):

- **Article 5**: a citizen's ethnic group may be confirmed and registered **only** on the basis of the father's or the mother's.
- **Article 6**: when a newborn is first registered, the police determine the ethnic group from the parents'; where the parents differ, from a joint application signed by both.
- **Article 20**: *"中国公民同外国人结婚生育或者依法收养的子女取得中国国籍的，其民族成份应当依据中国公民的民族成份确定"* — where a child of a Chinese citizen and a foreigner acquires Chinese nationality, the ethnic group **follows the Chinese parent**. So the child of a Han Chinese parent is registered as 汉族, not as "other".
- **Article 22** states that earlier documents inconsistent with the Measures are superseded, which is why older practice no longer applies.
- **Article 7** allows one change later: for a minor only in the listed family circumstances, and for an adult once within two years of turning 18, to the father's or the mother's group. The application goes to the county-level ethnic affairs department (Art. 11), not to the police.

**Children of overseas Chinese (华侨)**: Tianjin's separate procedure additionally requires, for ages 6 to under 18, a comparison against the national anti-trafficking DNA database, unless parentage has been confirmed by DNA testing [\[S41\]](https://ga.tj.gov.cn/bmfw/bszn/renkou/202202/t20220227_5815024.html).

## 3.3 Nationality Determination for Hukou Registration

If the child's nationality status is unclear (e.g., the child entered with a foreign passport), the public security bureau may require:

> *"持外国护照入境或申请人国籍情况存有疑义的，经设区市（含）以上公安机关出入境管理部门认定具有中国国籍的"* [\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm)

**Translation**: If the child entered with a foreign passport or the nationality status is in doubt, the exit-entry administration at the prefecture level or above must first **determine that the child has Chinese nationality** before *hukou* registration can proceed.

**Shanghai example**: Pudong New Area publishes a "nationality re-examination" (国籍认定) procedure for anyone whose nationality is in doubt and needs to be established, and for **under-18s applying for permanent residence**. For a child the materials are the authenticated birth certificate, the parents' passports or identity documents, the parents' domestic permanent residence certificate, the parents' marriage certificate (authenticated if foreign) and a written explanation from the parents. It is free and takes up to 60 working days from acceptance [\[S74\]](https://english.pudong.gov.cn/2017-07/17/c_84199.htm). The page dates from 2017, so confirm the current requirements before applying.

The Guangdong Public Security Department describes the general approach: a child with a nationality conflict needs a nationality determination—from the Chinese embassy/consulate if abroad, or from the prefecture-level exit-entry office where a parent has *hukou* or lives if in China. A child found to be Chinese can register *hukou* or obtain an Exit-Entry Permit in China, or a Travel Document abroad; a child found to be foreign is managed as a foreigner (visa or residence permit) [\[S39\]](http://gdga.gd.gov.cn/bsfw/zsk/qt/content/post_3368370.html).

## 3.4 What If the Child Already Has Hukou?

If the child already has *hukou* in China, this does **not** end the nationality conflict. A child with *hukou* may also hold a Chinese passport (see 4.2) [\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm)[\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm).

Some cities' registration rules ask for a declaration that the child has not acquired, or has given up, foreign citizenship or foreign permanent residency (e.g., Fuzhou) [\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm). See 3.2 on declarations and undertakings to give up the other nationality, and ask the registration office how a child who already holds a foreign passport will be treated before applying.

## 4.2 Can a Person with Nationality Conflict Get a Chinese Passport?

**Yes. There is no legal bar, but practice differs between consulates and localities.**

- **Legal position.** A nationality-conflict person is a Chinese national. The refusal ground in the Passport Law and the MPS measures, *"不具有中华人民共和国国籍的"* (does not have Chinese nationality), applies to non-nationals, so it does not bar them [\[S2\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zlbg/flfg/crjxg/200605/t20060512_961854.shtml)[\[S4\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1295795/content.html). The Exit-Entry Permit exists for those who find it **inconvenient (不便)** to use an ordinary passport [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html)[\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm). It is an alternative, not a prohibition.
- **Nagoya Consulate-General (June 2025):** passports and Travel Documents are both issued to Chinese citizens and do not change the holder's nationality status. A child who obtained a Chinese passport after returning to China with a Travel Document need not renew the Travel Document, and one person should not hold both at the same time [\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm).
- **UK Embassy:** China-born dual nationals who registered *hukou* and obtained a Chinese passport have the passport renewed; those without *hukou* are issued Travel Documents [\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm).
- **Abroad**, consulates generally issue Travel Documents rather than first-time passports to nationality-conflict persons. For example, Lahore describes first-time passports as mainly for people born abroad without a nationality conflict [\[S31\]](https://lahore.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsyw/lszj/hzlxz/201904/t20190419_5546339.htm)[\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm).

A Chinese passport does not by itself give the right to enter the country of the other nationality; see 4.5, 5.6, 7.2 and 7.3.

## 4.3 Applying for a Travel Document (Overseas)

**Who qualifies?**
> *"事实上存在国籍冲突的人员"* (Persons with de facto nationality conflicts) [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)[\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm)

**Specific categories** (Los Angeles Consulate wording [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm)):

1. Born abroad with a foreign nationality at birth; **both parents Chinese and neither** held foreign permanent residency at the time of birth.
2. Born abroad with a foreign nationality at birth; **one parent Chinese without** foreign permanent residency at the time of birth, the other parent foreign.
3. Born in China to one Chinese and one foreign parent, with a foreign nationality at birth under the foreign parent's nationality law.

If either Chinese parent held foreign permanent residency at the time of birth, the child does not qualify and applies for a visa instead [\[S6\]](https://gat.zj.gov.cn/art/2015/1/9/art_1229098253_600299.html)[\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm)[\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).

**Where to apply**

- **First application**: generally at the Chinese embassy/consulate in the child's **country of birth** [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)[\[S27\]](https://kw.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlxz/201901/t20190103_1648099.htm). The Kuwait Embassy accepts other cases only after consulting the post in the birth country [\[S27\]](https://kw.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlxz/201901/t20190103_1648099.htm).
- **Renewals and replacements**: at the embassy/consulate of the country where the person now lives, or at the MFA offices in Hong Kong and Macau [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)[\[S17\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/hzlxz/cjwd_660541/201805/t20180508_961052.shtml).
- **Hong Kong and Macau are a practical alternative to flying back to the country of birth.** Many families report applying for the **first** Travel Document there successfully: it is quicker, and with an early appointment it usually costs no more than an overnight stay, paying for express handling where it is offered.
  - **Macau's published scope covers first issuance outright.** The Commissioner's Office lists 旅行证颁发 for *"出生在国外或跨国婚姻所生子女首次申请旅行证"* — children born abroad or born of a cross-national marriage applying for the first time — alongside renewal and replacement, which take **4 working days with an optional express service** [\[S78\]](https://mo.ocmfa.gov.cn/chn/lsfw/lszj/zgrzjs/bzxz/202304/t20230428_11068694.htm).
  - **Hong Kong's published scope does not.** It lists renewal and replacement, both of which presuppose an existing document, plus a one-trip Travel Document for Chinese citizens stranded in Hong Kong without valid papers. Collection is on the 1st, 2nd or 3rd working day depending on service level, with no same-day option [\[S59\]](https://hk.ocmfa.gov.cn/lsxx/zgrzj/202310/t20231017_11162358.htm). **[Practice report]** Families nonetheless report first issues being accepted there, so practice may be ahead of the notice; confirm by email (fmcozgzj_hk@mfa.gov.cn) or phone (+852 3413 2404) before travelling for this purpose [\[S59\]](https://hk.ocmfa.gov.cn/lsxx/zgrzj/202310/t20231017_11162358.htm).
  - **Hong Kong also asks for a declaration about the Chinese passport.** The 《同意为未满16周岁申请人申办旅行证声明书》, signed by the guardian who accompanies the child, carries a tick-box — *"上述未成年申请人 □有 / □无 有效中国护照"* — and instructs that *"持有有效中国护照的，请自行取消旅行证颁发、换补发申请"*: anyone holding a valid Chinese passport must cancel the Travel Document application themselves in the 中国领事 APP [\[S79\]](https://hk.ocmfa.gov.cn/lsxx/bgxz/202504/P020250430586590270691.pdf). Macau applies the same principle: a valid passport holder may not apply for a Travel Document at the same time [\[S78\]](https://mo.ocmfa.gov.cn/chn/lsfw/lszj/zgrzjs/bzxz/202304/t20230428_11068694.htm).
- Some posts (e.g., Los Angeles) require the applicant to be physically inside their consular district [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).

**Application process**

- Submit through the **"中国领事" APP (China Consular Affairs APP)** and upload the documents [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)[\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).
- **Mind who the applicant is.** The APP's **face recognition** step belongs to setting up the account — it checks the adult creating it. The **applicant is the child**, and the child's details are what the application itself must carry. Mixing the two up is one of the commonest reasons these applications come back [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).
- **Video interviews are not the norm any more.** Los Angeles still calls minors to a video interview with a parent [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm); **[Practice report]** families currently report this only from US posts. It became standard during COVID, when posts were closed to the public; many have since returned to in-person applications with an optional mail-in and mail-back service. Check the page of the post that will handle your case rather than assuming.
- Appear in person or mail the originals when instructed [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).
- **[Practice report] Allow more time than the published figure.** The quoted processing times run from the day the papers reach the post, not from the day you post them. Families in the **United States** report three to four weeks door to door including both postal legs, which is awkward to fit inside a holiday. In **Germany (Munich, 2026)** documents were issued within three to four working days but then sat waiting to be mailed back. Where a trip is close, ask whether you can apply and collect **in person**; and note that **many posts offer no expedited or express service at all** — Hong Kong, for instance, has tiered collection days but no same-day option [\[S59\]](https://hk.ocmfa.gov.cn/lsxx/zgrzj/202310/t20231017_11162358.htm), while Macau and Calgary do offer an express tier [\[S78\]](https://mo.ocmfa.gov.cn/chn/lsfw/lszj/zgrzjs/bzxz/202304/t20230428_11068694.htm)[\[S72\]](https://calgary.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlx/202506/t20250619_11652989.htm).

**Typical first-issue materials** (Los Angeles [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm); similar lists at Kota Kinabalu [\[S30\]](https://kotakinabalu.china-consulate.gov.cn/chn/lsfw/lszj/hzlxz/202501/t20250108_11528793.htm), Kuching [\[S35\]](https://kuching.china-consulate.gov.cn/chn/lsfw/lszj/hzlxz/passport/202505/t20250529_11636374.htm), Jordan [\[S26\]](https://jo.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/gmfw/zytz/202002/t20200204_2508867.htm) and Kuwait [\[S27\]](https://kw.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlxz/201901/t20190103_1648099.htm)):

- Recent photo (white background).
- The child's birth certificate (or other proof of parentage).
- Both parents' valid passports.
- The residence permits or visas each parent held **at the time of the child's birth** (children born abroad).
- Household Register or Resident ID Card, if any (children born in China).
- The child's foreign passport, if any [\[S27\]](https://kw.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlxz/201901/t20190103_1648099.htm).
- For applicants **under 16**: one parent or legal guardian attends (or joins the video interview) and gives written consent [\[S26\]](https://jo.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/gmfw/zytz/202002/t20200204_2508867.htm)[\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm)[\[S30\]](https://kotakinabalu.china-consulate.gov.cn/chn/lsfw/lszj/hzlxz/202501/t20250108_11528793.htm).
- Los Angeles: both parents sign Part 1 of the 《国籍冲突人员办理旅行证声明书》, stating their nationality and that they held no foreign permanent residency at the time of birth [\[S24\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/cybg/hzlxzbg/202506/P020250625227680798412.pdf).
- Proof of address in the consular district (Los Angeles) [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).

**Calgary's current list for a first Travel Document** [\[S72\]](https://calgary.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlx/202506/t20250619_11652989.htm) is shorter and worth comparing: the application form, 2 photos, **both parents' passports**, **the foreign residence permits or visas both parents held at the time of the birth** (children born abroad only), and a birth certificate or other proof of parentage. It sets no age limit and asks for no entry-exit records. Calgary notes that processing time for a first issue depends on the case, because identity and nationality have to be checked.

**Processing**: Los Angeles normally issues within 4 working days after the documents arrive, longer when identity or nationality must be checked [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm). Calgary quotes 4 working days for a standard Travel Document, 3 expedited and 2 express, counted from the day the papers are handed in or the day after they arrive by post [\[S72\]](https://calgary.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlx/202506/t20250619_11652989.htm).

**Renewal (换发)**: Los Angeles renews when the Travel Document has expired, has less than 6 months left, has fewer than 4 blank visa pages, or when the holder left China on an Exit-Entry Permit [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm). Calgary's list is the same and adds early renewal where a new document is needed to apply for a visa or residence permit [\[S72\]](https://calgary.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlx/202506/t20250619_11652989.htm). **[Practice report]** Posts have also accepted renewals **more than 6 months** before expiry where there was a reason; this is discretionary, so confirm with the consular section by phone or email before booking anything around it.

**Using the Travel Document**: the holder enters China as a Chinese citizen without a visa [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm). It is intended mainly for travel to China; whether it can be used for third countries depends on the destination country [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)[\[S29\]](https://cl.china-embassy.gov.cn/lszlx/hzlxz/202001/t20200116_4818169.htm).

**[Practice report] Keep the two documents in their own lanes.** Everything outside China — booking the flight, checking in, entering and leaving the other country — should be done on the **foreign passport**, in the foreign name. The Travel Document's job is the Chinese border: it is what the child leaves and enters China on. Booking a ticket in the Travel Document's details invites mismatches at check-in and at the other country's border.

**What the "6 months" figure means**: many countries require a *passport* to have at least 6 months of validity left before you may travel. The 6 months that appears in Chinese consular guidance is a different thing — it is the point from which you become **eligible to apply for a new Travel Document**, not a minimum you must have left in order to travel. Los Angeles, Denmark and the Hong Kong office all use "expired, or less than 6 months remaining" as a renewal trigger [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm)[\[S59\]](https://hk.ocmfa.gov.cn/lsxx/zgrzj/202310/t20231017_11162358.htm).

The NIA's Border Inspection Department has confirmed this twice. Asked whether a Travel Document with under 6 months left had to be replaced before flying to China, it replied: *"出入境证件在有效期内可正常办理出入境手续。"* — exit-entry documents can be used normally for border formalities while within their validity [\[S69\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/preShowQuestion.jsp?MZ=guNQhpKb71r5uLbg%2fRAQWg%3d%3d). Asked the same question by a parent whose 4-year-old travels on a French passport and a Chinese Travel Document, it added: *"只要出入境当日该护照仍在签发有效期内，即为有效出入境证件。"* — as long as the document is still within its validity on the day of travel, it is a valid exit-entry document [\[S70\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/preShowQuestion.jsp?MZ=aNVvO1aUhKiIok8kVzENAA%3d%3d).

Airlines and third countries may still apply their own rules, so allow margin if a trip falls close to the expiry date.

## 4.4 Applying for an Exit-Entry Permit (Domestic)

**Legal basis**: Article 16 of the MPS 《中华人民共和国出入境通行证签发管理工作规范》 (公境出〔2007〕2223号), in force since 15 December 2007. The full 28-article text is published in the Guangdong Provincial Government's policy library [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html). The NIA's national service guide also lists nationality conflict as a ground for the permit, though its stated legal basis names only the published laws and MPS Order No. 96, not this 规范 [\[S9\]](https://s.nia.gov.cn/mps/bszy/gmcrg/sltxz/201903/t20190313_1010.html). Order No. 96 itself covers exit-entry permits only for border trade and border tourism and does not mention nationality conflict [\[S4\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1295795/content.html).

**Article 16 in full** [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html):
> *"申请人因国籍冲突，不便持用普通护照的，由其居住地设区市公安机关出入境管理机构受理、审批签发并制作三个月一次出入境有效的出入境通行证。居住地设区市公安机关出入境管理机构应当要求申请人提交外国护照、近期免冠照片一张以及填写完整的申请表，并通过公安部'全国法定不批准出境人员通报备案数据库'和'全国国家工作人员登记备案数据库'对其进行身份核查。有关审批签发、制作、不予签发出入境通行证等的程序、要求参照边境地区公安机关出入境管理机构签发出入境通行证的有关规定执行。"*

**Translation**: Where an applicant, because of a nationality conflict, is inconvenienced to hold an ordinary passport, the exit-entry administration of the **prefecture-level city of the applicant's place of residence** accepts, approves, issues and produces an Exit-Entry Permit **valid for one exit and one entry within 3 months**. That office must require the applicant to submit the **foreign passport, one recent bareheaded photo and a completed application form**, and must check the applicant's identity against the MPS databases of persons barred from leaving the country and of registered state functionaries. The procedures and requirements for approval, issuance, production and refusal follow those for permits issued by border-area exit-entry offices.

**What this means in practice**:

- **The national materials list is short**: foreign passport, one photo, application form. Cities that ask for more (see below) are adding local requirements.
- **No renewal or endorsement**: Article 21 provides that an Exit-Entry Permit cannot be endorsed or renewed (不予变更加注或者换发), and that except for the one-year multiple-entry type it cannot be replaced if lost (不予补发) [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html). Each trip therefore means a fresh application. Note that Dongguan's materials list nonetheless refers to renewing a permit [\[S37\]](http://gaj.dg.gov.cn/gb2021/ztzl/crjgl/crjxx/content/post_4350555.html).
- **Timing by reference**: the border-area rules applied by Article 16 give 15 days from acceptance, or 30 days where verification is needed (Art. 15) [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html). The faster local figures below are service commitments, not the legal maximum.
- **Exit and entry, not exit only**: Article 16 says 出入境有效 (valid for exit and entry). Articles 17 and 18, which cover Hong Kong and Macau cases, use 出境有效 (exit only) — a different category that is sometimes confused with this one (see 5.4) [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html).

**Required materials** (national list above; city lists vary):

- **Fujian** (baseline) [\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm): application form; photo; Resident ID Card (applicants with *hukou*), or the Household Register for under-16s without an ID card; the foreign passport and a copy. Materials supporting the reason for travel may be requested.
- **Dongguan** (longer list) [\[S37\]](http://gaj.dg.gov.cn/gb2021/ztzl/crjgl/crjxx/content/post_4350555.html):
  - Completed application form with photo
  - Birth certificate
  - The foreign passport held by the applicant
  - Both parents' identity documents and proof that one parent lives in Dongguan (e.g., Dongguan residence permit); not needed if a parent has Dongguan *hukou*
  - If entered using a Travel Document: the Travel Document
  - Copy of the latest entry stamp
  - If renewing: the original Exit-Entry Permit
  - If born abroad to Chinese parent(s): the parents' Chinese passports and the foreign visas/residence permits they held at the time of birth
  - Proof of travel to the country of the other nationality (e.g., flight ticket)
  - If *hukou* is registered: Resident ID Card and Household Register
  - Marriage certificate (if applicable)
  - Foreign documents must be authenticated and translated by a qualified agency

**Who applies**

- **Fujian**: the applicant in person; under-16s are accompanied by a guardian with proof of guardianship, or by an authorized companion holding the guardian's power of attorney [\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm).
- **Dongguan**: for applicants under 18, a guardian may apply on their behalf [\[S37\]](http://gaj.dg.gov.cn/gb2021/ztzl/crjgl/crjxx/content/post_4350555.html).

**[Practice report] Two local habits worth knowing about.**

- **Proof of travel to the other-nationality country.** Dongguan's list includes *"(ix) Supporting documents for travel to a country with conflicting nationality, such as airfare"* [\[S37\]](http://gaj.dg.gov.cn/gb2021/ztzl/crjgl/crjxx/content/post_4350555.html), and families report offices treating the permit as being **for the trip home** rather than for travel generally. That is a local addition: Article 16 requires only the foreign passport, a photo and the form [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html), and the Guangdong provincial guide asks merely for *"有关证明材料"* [\[S87\]](https://crj.gdga.gd.gov.cn/zwwbszy/zggmcrj/sqcrjtxz/202108/t20210816_1515.html). At the border itself, no family has reported being stopped or questioned on exit because the destination was not the country of the other nationality — in practice the permit works as a general exit document, whatever was written on the application.
- **Pressure to take a Chinese passport instead.** Some offices steer parents towards a Chinese passport, or issue the permit only grudgingly. A passport is lawful for a nationality-conflict person (see 4.2), but it brings the destination-visa problem described in 4.5, so it is not automatically the easier option.
- **Permits endorsed for one exit only.** The standard permit is valid for one exit **and** one entry (Art. 16) [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html), but families report permits used purely as an exit document — for example an adult French-Chinese dual national living in France who **enters** China on his Chinese passport and uses the permit only to **leave**, because his French passport carries no Schengen-area visa to show. Read what is endorsed on your own permit rather than assuming.

**Processing time**: Fujian 7 working days (5 if urgent) [\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm); Dongguan 5 working days (3 if urgent) [\[S37\]](http://gaj.dg.gov.cn/gb2021/ztzl/crjgl/crjxx/content/post_4350555.html); Hangzhou 7 working days (statutory limit 15) [\[S38\]](http://xd-sxkxtoss.oss-cn-hangzhou-zwynet-d01-a.internet.cloud.zj.gov.cn/2/f/%E5%87%BA%E5%85%A5%E5%A2%83%E9%80%9A%E8%A1%8C%E8%AF%81%E2%80%94%E5%9B%A0%E5%9B%BD%E7%B1%8D%E5%86%B2%E7%AA%81(%E6%9D%AD%E5%B7%9E%E5%B8%82%E5%85%AC%E5%AE%89%E5%B1%80).doc).

**Fee**: RMB 15 per permit (fee basis: 发改价格〔2017〕1186号) [\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm)[\[S37\]](http://gaj.dg.gov.cn/gb2021/ztzl/crjgl/crjxx/content/post_4350555.html).

| Can be held together with | A passport [\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm) | Not with a valid Chinese passport [\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm)[\[S58\]](https://hk.ocmfa.gov.cn/lsxx/zgrzj/202105/t20210513_8900963.htm) | Not with a Travel Document [\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm)[\[S58\]](https://hk.ocmfa.gov.cn/lsxx/zgrzj/202105/t20210513_8900963.htm) |

- **Exit-Entry Permit**: quick to obtain locally, but only one exit and one entry within 3 months, so every trip needs a new application.
- **Travel Document**: works much like a passport, but only for leaving and entering China. Chinese border inspection checks that the traveller holds a valid exit-entry document and, where the destination requires one, a visa — the NIA puts it as *"可持有效出入境证件及有效签证（免签除外）"* [\[S70\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/preShowQuestion.jsp?MZ=aNVvO1aUhKiIok8kVzENAA%3d%3d). A child traveling to the country of their other nationality is a citizen there, so there is no visa to show. **[Practice report]** Families report that this passes without difficulty. It cannot be obtained inside mainland China. In Hong Kong the current notice gives collection after 1, 2 or 3 working days depending on the service level, with no same-day option; the application must be filed in person by appointment and the old document is kept until collection, so the applicant has to wait in Hong Kong or pay for local return by courier [\[S59\]](https://hk.ocmfa.gov.cn/lsxx/zgrzj/202310/t20231017_11162358.htm). **[Practice report]** Mail-in applications at consulates abroad can take 3–4 weeks door to door; Los Angeles quotes 4 working days after the documents arrive [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).
- **On balance, the Travel Document is the least trouble** for a child living outside China: one document, multiple trips, no destination-visa question at the Chinese border, and none of the local friction described in 4.4. Its drawback is that it can only be obtained outside the mainland.
- **Chinese passport**: full Chinese-citizen documentation, but leaving China requires a visa or other entry permission for the destination (Exit-Entry Law Art. 9) [\[S3\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html). Home countries normally do not issue visas to their own citizens (US example [\[S63\]](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM020201.html)), so an Exit-Entry Permit—or a pro forma visa (7.3)—is usually still needed. One person should not hold a passport and a Travel Document at the same time; the Hong Kong office does not issue a Travel Document to someone holding a valid Chinese passport [\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm)[\[S58\]](https://hk.ocmfa.gov.cn/lsxx/zgrzj/202105/t20210513_8900963.htm).

# Part Five: Travel Scenarios and Requirements

## 5.1 Core Principle for Border Crossing

Chinese citizens leave and enter China with a passport or another Chinese travel document, and need a visa or other entry permission for the destination unless an exemption applies (Exit-Entry Law Art. 9) [\[S3\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html). They present these documents to border inspection (Art. 11), and a person without valid exit-entry documents is not allowed to leave (Art. 12) [\[S3\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html).

**[Practice report] The e-channel (自助通道) needs a Chinese passport.** Neither the Travel Document nor the Exit-Entry Permit carries the biometric chip the automated gates read, so both require a staffed counter. A foreign passport cannot be enrolled for the e-channel on the strength of holding a valid Travel Document or Exit-Entry Permit either — this was confirmed at the e-channel enrolment desk at Shanghai Pudong airport in 2025. Allow time for the manned queue, especially with children.

**Key rule**: If you are determined to be a Chinese national, you use Chinese documents (Passport, Travel Document, or Exit-Entry Permit) to enter and leave China. Visas are for foreigners, and a consulate that determines you are Chinese issues a Travel Document instead [\[S3\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html)[\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)[\[S33\]](http://wqb.hunan.gov.cn/xxgk/lbxx/201903/t20190320_5298474.html).

## 5.2 Scenario 1: Child Resides Outside China, Traveling to China

**Document needed**: **Travel Document** (旅行证)

- Validity: generally 2 years, multiple entries, unless endorsed otherwise [\[S10\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=NVp1yR02CHoC6IjdpMqppg%3D%3D)
- First application: usually at the embassy/consulate in the country of birth [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)
- The child enters China as a Chinese citizen—no visa required [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)

**If the Travel Document expires while in China**:

- Expiry does not generally affect living or studying in China [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm).
- To leave, apply to the local public security exit-entry office for a one-time Exit-Entry Permit, then renew the Travel Document at a Chinese embassy/consulate abroad [\[S17\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/hzlxz/cjwd_660541/201805/t20180508_961052.shtml)[\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm).

## 5.3 Scenario 2: Child Resides in China, Traveling Abroad

- **Valid Travel Document**: use it; it allows multiple exits and entries within its validity [\[S10\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=NVp1yR02CHoC6IjdpMqppg%3D%3D).
- **Hukou and a Chinese passport**: the passport may be used (see 4.2) [\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm). The UK Embassy notes that such persons have also left China on a one-time Exit-Entry Permit [\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm); see 4.5 and 7.3.
- **Otherwise**: **Exit-Entry Permit** (出入境通行证)
  - Validity: 3 months, **one exit and one entry** [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html)[\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm)
  - Apply at the prefecture-level public security exit-entry office where you live [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html)
  - Each trip needs a new application. The MPS rule provides only this 3-month permit for nationality-conflict cases [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html); the multi-entry permits in the MPS measures are for border trade and border tourism [\[S4\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1295795/content.html).

**Coming back**:

- Returning **within** the permit's 3-month validity: the same permit covers the return entry [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html)[\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm).
- Staying abroad **longer**: apply for a Travel Document at the Chinese embassy/consulate abroad. Los Angeles treats a person who left China on an Exit-Entry Permit as a Travel Document renewal case [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).
- The MFA and the Jamaica Embassy describe the permit issued after a Travel Document expires as a one-time document for leaving China [\[S17\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/hzlxz/cjwd_660541/201805/t20180508_961052.shtml)[\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm). Always check the validity endorsed on the permit itself.

## 5.4 Scenario 3: Travel Document Expired While in China

A Travel Document that expires while the child is in China does not affect living or studying there; it affects leaving. The document issued for the departure is the **Exit-Entry Permit**, valid for **one exit and one entry within 3 months** under Article 16 of the MPS 规范 [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html). The exit-only form of the permit (三个月一次出境有效) belongs to Articles 17 and 18, which cover Hong Kong and Macau document cases rather than nationality conflict [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html) — worth knowing, because the two are easily confused, and what counts is the endorsement on the permit in your hand.

**The route out and back**: the MFA and the Jamaica Embassy advise applying to the local public security office for a one-time Exit-Entry Permit to leave, then renewing the Travel Document abroad—at a Chinese embassy/consulate in the country where the child lives, or at the MFA offices in Hong Kong/Macau [\[S17\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/hzlxz/cjwd_660541/201805/t20180508_961052.shtml)[\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm).

## 5.5 Scenario 4: Foreign Passport plus Chinese Visa?

**This is not available.** The consulate decides whether a child receives a visa or a Travel Document, based on the place of birth, the foreign nationality and the parents' residence status at the time of birth; parents cannot choose [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm). A person the consulate determines to be Chinese is issued a Travel Document, not a visa in the foreign passport [\[S33\]](http://wqb.hunan.gov.cn/xxgk/lbxx/201903/t20190320_5298474.html)[\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm).

**[Practice report] A visa in the passport is not proof that there is no conflict.** Visas have been issued to children who were in fact Chinese nationals, where the post did not know of or did not pursue the Chinese nationality — there is no provision for it, but it happens. A visa obtained that way settles nothing: nationality is determined afresh each time documents are applied for [\[S12\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=XnD%2Fs0j%2FZ6YV1zBzlKiEEg%3D%3D), and the question can resurface at the next application, at *hukou* registration, or at the border. If you think this describes your child, work out the position before the next trip rather than during it.

## 5.6 Scenario 5: Traveling Between China and the US, Canada, Australia or New Zealand

China's consular treaties with the **United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand** contain a clause facilitating travel between the two countries for persons who claim both nationalities, without recognizing dual nationality [\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm). The China–US exchange of notes (17 September 1980) specifies that exit procedures and documents follow the law of the country of residence, and entry procedures and documents follow the law of the destination country [\[S7\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/gywm/lxlqz/201710/t20171020_5539493.htm). See 7.2. No comparable clause was found for other countries (e.g., France). Some families also use pro forma visas or right-of-abode certificates; see 7.3.

## 5.7 Scenario 6: Visa-Free Entry with the Foreign Passport?

**Caveat: no official statement specifically addressing nationality-conflict persons was found.** The following is derived from the law:

- The Exit-Entry Law defines a foreigner as a person without Chinese nationality—*"外国人，是指不具有中国国籍的人"* (Art. 89)—and its visa exemptions (Art. 22) apply to foreigners [\[S3\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html).
- Embassy FAQs describe China's unilateral visa-free policy as applying to foreigners holding ordinary passports [\[S34\]](https://au.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/qz/202511/t20251112_11751551.htm).
- The authoritative lists are published centrally, and change often: the MFA's **《中外互免签证协定一览表》** covers the mutual visa-exemption agreements, with the categories of passport each one applies to [\[S80\]](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/wjbzwfwpt/kzx/tzgg/202504/t20250414_11594222.html), and the NIA publishes the **transit visa-free** policy and the countries it applies to [\[S81\]](https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202506/content_7027403.htm). Check these before relying on any list, including this guide's.
- Chinese citizens must leave and enter China with a passport or other Chinese travel document (Art. 9) [\[S3\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html).

A person China regards as a Chinese national should therefore not expect to use visa-free or transit visa-free entry with a foreign passport. Confirm with the consulate, or with the local exit-entry office (hotline 12367), before travelling [\[S37\]](http://gaj.dg.gov.cn/gb2021/ztzl/crjgl/crjxx/content/post_4350555.html).

**[Practice report]** Some young adults who grew up in China registered only as foreigners (never with *hukou*, only with residence permits in the foreign passport, see 7.6) have later, after moving abroad for university, entered China under the visa-free arrangements for EU countries. This does not change the legal position above: nationality is determined when documents are applied for [\[S11\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=pkmAunjGDGzbiy1XkMpfaA%3D%3D)[\[S12\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=XnD%2Fs0j%2FZ6YV1zBzlKiEEg%3D%3D), and a later determination could still treat the person as Chinese. For French passport holders, the French government lists the visa exemption (stays of up to 30 days) as valid until 31 December 2026 [\[S64\]](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/information-par-pays/chine/conseils-aux-voyageurs-entree-sejour).

# Part Six: Adulthood (Age 18 and Above)

## 6.1 Does Nationality Conflict Automatically End at 18?

**No.** The nationality conflict does **not** end automatically at 18.

The **National Immigration Administration (国家移民管理局)** replied [\[S11\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=pkmAunjGDGzbiy1XkMpfaA%3D%3D):
> *"相关子女向中国主管部门申请办理出入境证件时，有关部门将会根据具体情况对其国籍进行认定。"*

**Translation**: When such children apply to Chinese authorities for exit-entry documents, the relevant departments will determine their nationality based on the specific circumstances.

- In another reply, the NIA said that China does not recognize the foreign nationality of a person who is Chinese under the law, and that the exit-entry office determines the person's nationality before issuing the corresponding documents [\[S12\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=XnD%2Fs0j%2FZ6YV1zBzlKiEEg%3D%3D).
- The Jamaica Embassy states that nationality-conflict children who applied for and used Travel Documents continue to do so as adults [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm).
- A grandparent asked specifically about the rumour that *"18周岁以后旅行证自动失效，必须办理中国签证"* — that a Travel Document lapses at 18 and a Chinese visa becomes necessary. The NIA did not confirm it, replying only with the general rule: a Travel Document is usually valid for 2 years with multiple exits and entries, unless the observations page is endorsed for a single trip or for return only [\[S10\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=NVp1yR02CHoC6IjdpMqppg%3D%3D).
- The most recent reply on the point (September 2026) concerned a person born in China, foreign by descent, who had lived there all along on Travel Documents and had now reached adulthood. The NIA quoted Article 9 and declined to decide the case: *"因您未详细说明具体情况，关于是否存在自动丧失中国国籍的问题，建议您向所在地公安机关出入境管理部门咨询。"* — because the circumstances were not set out in detail, the question of automatic loss should be put to the local exit-entry authority [\[S67\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/preShowQuestion.jsp?MZ=kMbAO3DWoMtvs6ew0LR%2bkQ%3d%3d). Reaching 18 was not treated as the trigger; settlement abroad plus voluntary acquisition is.

**Two ways to lose Chinese nationality** [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html)[\[S11\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=pkmAunjGDGzbiy1XkMpfaA%3D%3D):

1. **Article 9 of the Nationality Law**: Settle abroad and voluntarily acquire foreign nationality → automatic loss
2. **Article 11 of the Nationality Law**: Apply to renounce Chinese nationality and receive approval

If **neither** applies, the person **retains Chinese nationality** and continues to use Chinese documents (Passport, Travel Document or Exit-Entry Permit).

**Consular practice differs.** The Edinburgh Consulate-General states that a child who had Chinese nationality at birth automatically loses it if the child later settles abroad while holding a foreign nationality [\[S22\]](https://edinburgh.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlxz/202308/t20230824_11132135.htm). The UK Embassy's reference table likewise distinguishes later naturalization with foreign permanent residency (visa) from naturalization without it (Travel Document) [\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm). Check with the consulate for your district.

## 6.2 Can an Adult Apply for a Travel Document?

**Yes.** The Los Angeles Consulate has a category for first-time applicants aged **16 or over** who have never held a Chinese travel document, using the same parentage criteria as for children [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm). Its declaration form covers:
> *"申请人为未满16周岁的未成年人 ... 或首次办理的成年人"* (Applicants who are minors under 16 ... or adults applying for the first time) [\[S24\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/cybg/hzlxzbg/202506/P020250625227680798412.pdf)

**For adults applying for a Travel Document**:

- Apply personally; no guardian accompaniment.
- For a **first** application, the parents still complete Part 1 of the declaration, covering their nationality and residence status at the time of birth [\[S24\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/cybg/hzlxzbg/202506/P020250625227680798412.pdf). Applicants aged 16 or over who are renewing complete Part 2 themselves [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).
- Face recognition in the APP applies to all applicants [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).
- No entry-exit records are required. An earlier version of this guide attributed such a requirement to the Calgary Consulate; Calgary's current materials list asks only for the form, photos, both parents' passports, the parents' residence documents from the time of the birth and proof of parentage [\[S72\]](https://calgary.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlx/202506/t20250619_11652989.htm), and nothing of the kind appears at Los Angeles either [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm). The list being built around the **parents'** documents is also why they complete the declaration even when the applicant is an adult.

## 6.3 Can an Adult Apply for an Exit-Entry Permit?

**Yes.** The MPS rule sets no age condition [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html). In Fujian, applicants aged 16 or over apply in person; under-16s are accompanied by a guardian [\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm).

## 6.4 What If the Adult Has Hukou?

*Hukou* does not by itself change nationality status. A person with *hukou* may hold a Chinese passport (see 4.2) [\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm)[\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm); otherwise the person continues to use a Travel Document abroad or an Exit-Entry Permit in China. Fujian's permit guide lists the Resident ID Card among the materials for applicants with *hukou*, so having *hukou* does not exclude the permit [\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm).

## 6.5 Can an Adult Renounce Chinese Nationality?

**Yes**, under certain conditions.

**《国籍法》第十条 (Nationality Law Article 10)** [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html): A Chinese citizen may apply to renounce Chinese nationality if they meet **one** of the following conditions:

1. A close relative who is a foreign national
2. Settled abroad
3. Other legitimate reasons

**Who cannot renounce** (Article 12) [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html):
> *"国家工作人员和现役军人，不得退出中国国籍。"* (State functionaries and active-duty military personnel shall not renounce Chinese nationality.)

**Procedure** [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html):

- **Minors**: applications for persons under 18 **may** be filed by their parents or other legal representatives (Art. 14).
- **Where**: in China, the local city or county public security bureau; abroad, a Chinese embassy or consulate (Art. 15).
- **Approval**: by the Ministry of Public Security, which issues a certificate (Art. 16). Nationality is lost once the application is approved (Art. 11).
- **Afterwards**: return any Chinese passport or Exit-Entry Permit to the public security office or a Chinese embassy/consulate [\[S4\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1295795/content.html), and travel to China with a foreign passport and a Chinese visa [\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm).
- **Staying on after approval**: a person whose renunciation is approved becomes a foreigner and needs a foreigner's document to remain. The NIA states that *"经批准退出中国国籍需要在中国境内停留的外国人，公安出入境管理部门可以签发停留期不超过180日的停留证件"* — a stay permit of up to 180 days, to allow a short stay and departure — and that where leaving is impossible for reasons such as suspended flights, a further stay permit can be applied for with supporting evidence [\[S84\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=d1FaxlwTDPPcm7Xn7A5qvw%3D%3D). The national service guide for stay permits sets out the procedure [\[S86\]](https://s.nia.gov.cn/mps/mbbszy/wgrcrj/201903/t20190316_1016.html). Plan the next status before the renunciation is approved, not after.

**Minors in practice**:

- National law lets parents or other legal representatives apply for under-18s (Art. 14) [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html), and official practice accepts minors. The NIA answered a German family whose child — born in Germany in 2004, never resident in China, with no *hukou*, no Chinese passport and no Travel Document — that *"对于未办理落户手续及中国出入境证件的退出中国国籍申请人可不提供户籍及中国出入境证件证明材料"*: an applicant with no *hukou* and no Chinese documents simply does not supply those papers, and the detailed list comes from **the exit-entry office of the parents' place of *hukou*** [\[S82\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=tHT%2FAYmaaxaT7ESnJwTfYQ%3D%3D). No age condition appears anywhere in that answer. The Singapore Embassy requires a legal guardian to accompany an applicant under 18 [\[S61\]](https://sg.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/lsfw_0/lszj/GJSQ/202309/t20230913_11142229.htm), and the NIA's own guide directs applicants in China to the exit-entry office of the place of *hukou* [\[S57\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n1051057/n1051076/c1051258/content.html).
- **[Practice report] (second-hand) Shanghai**: other families report that renunciation is not possible before age 18. No written Shanghai rule to this effect was found, and no first-hand account of a refusal was available, so treat this as a rumour to check rather than a rule: national law and the NIA's own answers do accept applications for minors [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html)[\[S82\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=tHT%2FAYmaaxaT7ESnJwTfYQ%3D%3D)[\[S61\]](https://sg.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/lsfw_0/lszj/GJSQ/202309/t20230913_11142229.htm). A 2017 French Senate question reported that local administrations told Franco-Chinese families they did not know the procedure for giving up Chinese nationality, which is consistent with inconsistent local handling rather than a fixed age limit [\[S62\]](https://www.senat.fr/questions/base/2017/qSEQ170224983.html). No renunciation service item could be found in Shanghai's 一网通办 service catalogue either, while other authorities do publish guidance [\[S61\]](https://sg.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/lsfw_0/lszj/GJSQ/202309/t20230913_11142229.htm)[\[S57\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n1051057/n1051076/c1051258/content.html) — an absence that fits the reports but proves nothing on its own. The Shanghai police have themselves urged people who settled abroad and took another nationality to complete renunciation, pointing them to Chinese embassies and consulates abroad [\[S89\]](https://gaj.sh.gov.cn/shga/vWzqWzXxfbGj/jcdtDetail?pa=110ef360e4374a4100cb27225b222e3d8da220d97f60089fc65075be340056bd). Before planning around this, ask the Shanghai exit-entry authority directly (12367).
- This matters for permanent residence, which children can obtain through a parent only until 18 (see 6.6).

## 6.6 Chinese Permanent Residence ("China Green Card") for Children

- **Only for foreigners**: permanent residence is a status for foreigners (Exit-Entry Law Art. 47), and a foreigner is a person without Chinese nationality (Art. 89) [\[S3\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html). A child whom China regards as Chinese must first lose Chinese nationality, in practice by renunciation (6.5).
- **Parent-child category (亲子团聚)**: unmarried children under 18 joining their parents are one of the national categories [\[S53\]](https://s.nia.gov.cn/mps/bszy/wgrcrj/yjjl/201903/t20190313_1008.html), set out in the 《外国人在中国永久居留审批管理办法》 [\[S88\]](https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/gryjzgjj/20240823/ca2233bde26c451c8820507f62e1483f.html). The child must be **confirmed by a nationality review to hold foreign nationality** — the procedure described in 3.3, which lists under-18 permanent-residence applicants among the people it is for [\[S74\]](https://english.pudong.gov.cn/2017-07/17/c_84199.htm).
- **[Practice report] Expect the nationality paperwork to be the hard part.** A Shanghai parent whose daughter had naturalised in Japan as a child told the NIA that the counter demanded a *"退出中国国籍证明"* and would accept neither the Osaka consulate's stamp in the old Chinese passport nor the police station's *hukou* cancellation certificate. The NIA replied with Articles 9 and 10 — automatic loss on voluntarily acquiring a foreign nationality while settled abroad, and approved renunciation — and referred the case to the Shanghai exit-entry bureau to handle [\[S83\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=7PAMM9ENucz7UFIpR1YcHw%3D%3D). If you meet the same wall, the documents that establish loss of Chinese nationality are the point, and the case can be escalated.
- **Where to apply**: the exit-entry office of the prefecture-level city (in municipalities, the district or county public security bureau) [\[S53\]](https://s.nia.gov.cn/mps/bszy/wgrcrj/yjjl/201903/t20190313_1008.html). In Shanghai the file is taken by the Municipal Immigration Service Center or a district exit-entry office before going to the NIA; Shanghai's own service page for this category was withdrawn while this version was being prepared, so check the current procedure with the office before assembling papers. **[Practice report]** The application follows the Chinese parent and is made where that parent's *hukou* is registered.
- **Timing risk**: because the category ends at 18, a child who cannot renounce before 18 (reported Shanghai practice, 6.5) would no longer qualify by the time renunciation becomes possible.

# Part Seven: Special Considerations

## 6.7 Restoring Chinese Nationality

A person who has lost Chinese nationality is not shut out permanently.

- **Article 13**: a foreigner who once held Chinese nationality may apply to **restore** it for legitimate reasons. A person whose restoration is approved **may not keep the foreign nationality** [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html).
- **Where to apply**: the NIA states that a restoration application may be made to the exit-entry administration at the place of residence, the former place of *hukou*, **or** the (former) place of *hukou* of a Chinese parent [\[S71\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/preShowQuestion.jsp?MZ=wgNJcOzwo4YrthnBTxYcFg%3d%3d). The applicant need not return to the original *hukou* city.
- **Giving up the other nationality is not a Chinese procedure.** Asked by a Canadian citizen living in China how to drop the foreign nationality and keep the Chinese one, the NIA answered only with Article 9 (automatic loss on voluntarily acquiring a foreign nationality while settled abroad) and Article 13 (restoration, with no keeping of the foreign nationality) [\[S85\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=9PQAMhZWr6Zk9fibcZHJMA%3D%3D) — in other words, there is no Chinese form for renouncing a foreign nationality; that is the other country's business, and China's route is restoration.
- Article 7 naturalization (see 1.3) is the parallel route for a person who never held Chinese nationality, and carries the same bar on keeping the foreign one [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html)[\[S6\]](https://gat.zj.gov.cn/art/2015/1/9/art_1229098253_600299.html).

## 7.1 Hukou and Exit-Entry Permits

Having *hukou*, or not having it, does not decide whether the Exit-Entry Permit can be issued. The sources that can be checked all point the same way:

- The MPS rule for the permit sets no *hukou* condition [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html).
- Fujian's guide lists the Resident ID Card among the materials for applicants who have *hukou* [\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm), and Dongguan's lists the ID card and Household Register for applicants whose *hukou* is registered [\[S37\]](http://gaj.dg.gov.cn/gb2021/ztzl/crjgl/crjxx/content/post_4350555.html).
- The Guangdong Public Security Department says a child determined to be Chinese can register *hukou* **or** apply for an Exit-Entry Permit in China [\[S39\]](http://gdga.gd.gov.cn/bsfw/zsk/qt/content/post_3368370.html).
- Local *hukou* registration rules may still ask about foreign citizenship (e.g., Fuzhou's declaration) [\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm).
- Article 16 of the 规范 sets out the whole national materials list — foreign passport, photo, application form — and says nothing about *hukou* in either direction [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html).
- Asked whether a child with a Travel Document but no *hukou* and no Resident ID Card is nonetheless Chinese, the NIA answered with Article 5 alone [\[S68\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/preShowQuestion.jsp?MZ=1TIylb4HoPcp7vWV49%2f6Lw%3d%3d).

## 7.2 Consular-Treaty Travel Facilitation

The previous version of this section described "pro forma visas" placed in Chinese passports. No Chinese government source for that practice was found, so the section now covers the treaty provisions that do exist.

- **Which countries**: China's consular treaties with the United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand each contain a clause under which both sides facilitate travel between the two countries for persons who claim both nationalities, without recognizing dual nationality [\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm).
- **United States**: the supplementary exchange of notes to the China–US Consular Treaty (17 September 1980) states that this facilitation *"并不意味着两国政府承认双重国籍"* (does not mean either government recognizes dual nationality). Exit procedures and documents follow the law of the country of residence; entry procedures and documents follow the law of the destination country [\[S7\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/gywm/lxlqz/201710/t20171020_5539493.htm).
- **What it does not do**: the clause facilitates travel. It does not create recognized dual nationality, and it does not replace the Chinese document rules in Parts Four and Five.
- **Other countries**: no comparable clause was found (e.g., France). Families with other nationalities should follow Parts Four and Five.

## 7.3 Pro Forma Visas and Right-of-Abode Certificates

This section covers the documents the **other** country can put in a Chinese passport so that Chinese exit control sees permission to enter the destination. No Chinese source describes any of this; the material below comes from US and UK government rules, a US immigration law firm, and practice reports.

- **Principle**: countries normally do not issue visas to their own citizens. US rules prohibit issuing a visa or other documentation to a US citizen for entry into the United States as a noncitizen [\[S63\]](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM020201.html).
- **United States**: a US immigration law firm reports that US posts in China issue a "pro forma" visa in the Chinese passport of a US–China dual-national **minor**, as a courtesy and solely to let the child leave China for the US; the child still enters the US on the US passport. Adults are generally not considered. The practice is not in US regulations or the public Foreign Affairs Manual; since 2024 such visas have reportedly been issued as 10-year multiple-entry visas that look like ordinary B1/B2 visas [\[S65\]](https://lawandborder.com/u-s-consulate-shenyang-pro-forma-visas-dual-nationals/). **[Practice report]** Not every post issues them, and applications are not always approved.
- **France**: **[Practice report] (second-hand)** French consulates are reported to have issued comparable visas to Franco-Chinese minors, and not to issue them after 18, so that from then on an Exit-Entry Permit or Travel Document plus the French passport is needed. No French government source and no first-hand account confirm this; France Diplomatie's China page does not mention the practice [\[S64\]](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/information-par-pays/chine/conseils-aux-voyageurs-entree-sejour). Ask the French consulate handling your district before relying on it.
- **Chinese side**: the Chinese passport carrying the pro forma visa is used to leave and re-enter China (see 4.5). No Chinese source was found on how border inspection treats these visas.

**United Kingdom: certificate of entitlement to the right of abode.** Where a pro forma visa is a discretionary courtesy, the UK has a published equivalent that can sometimes be used the same way [\[S66\]](https://www.gov.uk/right-of-abode/apply-for-a-certificate-of-entitlement):

- **What it is**: official proof that the holder has the right of abode in the UK. It is not a visa and does not expire.
- **Who can apply**: a British citizen holding a foreign passport (or a Commonwealth citizen with the right of abode). It cannot be held together with a valid British passport — an existing British passport has to be surrendered.
- **Cost and time**: £589; roughly 3 weeks after the appointment when applying from outside the UK, about 8 weeks inside the UK.
- **Format change**: certificates are now issued **digitally** through a UKVI account rather than as a sticker in the foreign passport. An existing sticker (vignette) may still be used until that passport expires, and can be converted to digital for free.
- **Caveat for use at Chinese exit control**: the value of this route for leaving China rested on the sticker being physically visible in the Chinese passport. Whether a digital certificate shown as a share code satisfies Chinese border inspection or an airline check-in desk is **not established** — no Chinese source addresses it. Anyone planning to rely on it should test it with the airline and the local exit-entry authority (12367) before booking, and give up a British passport only after confirming this works.

## 7.4 Foreign Documents: Legalization and Apostille

- Since **7 November 2023**, public documents from other Apostille Convention member states need only an **apostille** from the issuing country for use in mainland China; legalization by the Chinese embassy/consulate is no longer required for them. Documents from non-member countries still go through consular legalization [\[S19\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/gyls/lsgz/fwxx/202310/t20231023_11166241.shtml).
- *Hukou* offices accept the foreign birth certificate with consular legalization, an apostille or a notarial certificate, plus a translation by a qualified agency (Fuzhou) [\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm). Dongguan requires foreign documents for the Exit-Entry Permit to be authenticated and translated by a qualified agency [\[S37\]](http://gaj.dg.gov.cn/gb2021/ztzl/crjgl/crjxx/content/post_4350555.html).

## 7.5 What About DNA Testing for Hukou Registration?

**Why it comes up at all.** *Hukou* registration is the moment the state records who a child's parents are, and the birth certificate is the proof it normally relies on — Shanghai calls the certificate *"证明新生儿出生状态、血亲关系以及申报出生登记的法定医学证明"*, the legal medical proof of birth, blood relationship and the basis for birth registration [\[S76\]](https://www.shhuangpu.gov.cn/zw/009002/009002016/009002016002/009002016002001/20150713/f0a0214b-dcb2-4252-99f9-2264f6bd8ab9.html). A parentage test is what the authorities fall back on when that chain of proof is broken or cannot be checked, which happens more often in these families than in others:

- the birth happened **abroad**, so no Chinese birth certificate exists and the foreign one is a translated document from another system;
- the parents' names or details on the certificate **do not match** their identity documents (Shanghai requires a *hukou*-office certificate in that case, and a parentage report where necessary) [\[S76\]](https://www.shhuangpu.gov.cn/zw/009002/009002016/009002016002/009002016002001/20150713/f0a0214b-dcb2-4252-99f9-2264f6bd8ab9.html);
- **years have passed** since the birth before registration was applied for, which is why several cities set an age threshold;
- the child is registered on the basis of **one parent** only, or the family relationship cannot be shown from the Household Register.

It is a proof-of-parentage requirement, not a check on nationality or on the child's other passport. Local rules differ widely:

- **Tianjin, children born abroad**: a parentage (DNA) test certificate is required for ages 6 to under 18, with review up to the district sub-bureau (quoted in 3.2) [\[S40\]](https://ga.tj.gov.cn/bmfw/bszn/renkou/202202/t20220227_5815023.html).
- **Tianjin, children of overseas Chinese (华侨)**: ages 6 to under 18 are also checked against the national anti-trafficking DNA database, unless parentage has been confirmed by DNA testing [\[S41\]](https://ga.tj.gov.cn/bmfw/bszn/renkou/202202/t20220227_5815024.html).
- **Shanghai**: no age-based requirement; a parentage report is needed only in listed cases (see 3.2) [\[S46\]](https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/gwk/search/content/GKXX-20230426101209846--2642)[\[S47\]](https://zwdt.sh.gov.cn/govPortals/bsfw/item/086a73b2-d5f6-4dff-b33a-cc8f1bbab381).
- Other cities set their own rules.
- **Which institution may test.** Not any laboratory: it must be a registered judicial appraisal institution. Under Article 6 of the NPC Standing Committee's 2005 Decision on judicial appraisal, provincial justice departments examine applicants, register those who qualify, and *"编入鉴定人和鉴定机构名册并公告"* — enter them in the roster of appraisers and institutions and publish it [\[S73\]](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2005/content_63181.htm). The roster is kept and published **by each provincial justice department**; there is no single national directory site, and an earlier version of this guide named one that does not exist under that name. Before paying for a test, check that the institution appears on the roster for the province where you will register, and that the report is issued as a 司法鉴定意见书 rather than a consumer "peace of mind" test, which registration offices do not accept.

## 7.6 Children Registered as Foreigners (Residence Permits)

**[Practice report] Shanghai, former practice**: Shanghai is reported to have issued China-born dual-national children a dependent-type residence permit in the foreign passport, following the foreign parent. At 18 the young person had to switch to a study-based permit, usually arranged through an international school; after graduation they had to leave China or enrol again (e.g., at a local university or in a Chinese-language programme) to stay. A family-reunion (Q1) visa from a Chinese consulate abroad should also be possible.

**The period is not established.** No start or end date for this practice could be confirmed, and no official Shanghai document describing it was found. It also sits awkwardly with the 2008 Shanghai Public Security Bureau answer below, which treated a Shanghai-born child of one Chinese parent holding a foreign passport as Chinese [\[S50\]](https://gaj.sh.gov.cn/shga/wzXxfbGj/detail?pa=110ef360e4374a41ba22608fbc632f46ed96736af54c5a8198d21bb8618abb68). Anyone currently holding such a permit should confirm their position with the Shanghai exit-entry authority (12367) rather than assume the arrangement continues.

**What the official rules say**:

- Residence permits are for foreigners; the issuing office must confirm that the applicant has foreign nationality [\[S54\]](https://s.nia.gov.cn/mps/bszy/wgrcrj/sqwgrjlzj/201903/t20190313_1007.html).
- Private-affairs (私人事务类) permits cover the spouse, parents, parents-in-law and **under-18 children** of foreigners residing in China for work or study, for the same period as that family member's permit [\[S54\]](https://s.nia.gov.cn/mps/bszy/wgrcrj/sqwgrjlzj/201903/t20190313_1007.html)[\[S55\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/wgrlh/lhqz/sblhqz_660598/201307/t20130722_961423.shtml). The child category ends at 18.
- Study (学习类) permits require a letter from the school and run for the study period [\[S54\]](https://s.nia.gov.cn/mps/bszy/wgrcrj/sqwgrjlzj/201903/t20190313_1007.html).
- Family reunion (Q1 visa, 团聚类 permit) covers family members of Chinese citizens—including adult children—with an invitation from the Chinese family member living in China [\[S55\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/wgrlh/lhqz/sblhqz_660598/201307/t20130722_961423.shtml)[\[S56\]](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/wgrlh/lhqz/cjwdn_660600/201407/t20140717_961499.shtml).
- **For a visit rather than a stay, a visa may not be needed at all.** Where the person is treated as a foreign national of a country covered by a mutual visa-exemption agreement, or by the unilateral or transit visa-free arrangements, a family visit falls inside those schemes on the same terms as any other short visit, within the stay each one allows [\[S80\]](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/wjbzwfwpt/kzx/tzgg/202504/t20250414_11594222.html)[\[S81\]](https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202506/content_7027403.htm)[\[S34\]](https://au.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/qz/202511/t20251112_11751551.htm). That is a route for relatives visiting a family in China; it is **not** a way round a nationality conflict for the child, because a person China regards as its own national is outside the foreigner rules altogether (see 5.7) [\[S12\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=XnD%2Fs0j%2FZ6YV1zBzlKiEEg%3D%3D). Permits for under-18s run at most 3 years and not beyond the 18th birthday; others up to 2 years [\[S54\]](https://s.nia.gov.cn/mps/bszy/wgrcrj/sqwgrjlzj/201903/t20190313_1007.html).
- In 2008 the Shanghai Public Security Bureau answered that a Shanghai-born child with one Chinese parent who had obtained a foreign passport from a foreign consulate in China has Chinese nationality and should follow the nationality-conflict exit procedure [\[S50\]](https://gaj.sh.gov.cn/shga/wzXxfbGj/detail?pa=110ef360e4374a41ba22608fbc632f46ed96736af54c5a8198d21bb8618abb68).
- A 2017 French Senate question reported that Franco-Chinese children born in China could no longer renew their visas because China regarded them as Chinese [\[S62\]](https://www.senat.fr/questions/base/2017/qSEQ170224983.html).
**What a nationality-conflict child does *not* need in order to stay in China**

A child the authorities treat as a Chinese national sits outside the foreigner regime, which has a practical consequence parents often find surprising:

- **No foreigner registration at the police station.** The accommodation-registration duty in Article 39 of the Exit-Entry Law is a duty of **foreigners**, and Article 89 defines a foreigner as a person without Chinese nationality [\[S3\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html). A child determined to be Chinese is not one. **[Practice report]** In practice such registration is usually impossible anyway, as there is no visa or residence permit to register against. It can sometimes be forced through a municipal or the newer NIA online registration system using the foreign passport, but that records the child as something they are not; better avoided.
- **No *hukou*, and no local residence permit, in order to remain.** Neither is a condition of staying: *hukou* is a residence registration for Chinese citizens, and the foreigners' residence permit presupposes foreign nationality [\[S3\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html). **[Practice report]** Children in this position live in China indefinitely without either, including in cities other than the parents'. What the lack of *hukou* does affect is access to services — public school places, insurance, and so on — which is the usual reason families register it (see Part Three).
- **No visa, and no permitted period of stay.** Having entered as a Chinese national on a Travel Document or Exit-Entry Permit, there is no stay period to overrun and nothing to extend; the Travel Document's expiry affects the next **departure**, not the right to be there (see 5.2) [\[S12\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=XnD%2Fs0j%2FZ6YV1zBzlKiEEg%3D%3D).

- Being known to Chinese systems as a foreigner does not settle nationality: authorities determine nationality when the person applies for documents [\[S11\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=pkmAunjGDGzbiy1XkMpfaA%3D%3D)[\[S12\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=XnD%2Fs0j%2FZ6YV1zBzlKiEEg%3D%3D). See 5.7 on visa-free entry.

# Part Eight: Summary and Quick Reference

## 8.1 Document Decision Tree

```
1. Does the person have Chinese nationality?
   (decided by the Chinese embassy/consulate abroad, or by the
    prefecture-level exit-entry office in China – see 1.3 and 3.3)
   ├── NO  → Foreign passport + Chinese visa (or other entry permission for foreigners)
   └── YES → go to 2

2. Where is the person now?
   ├── OUTSIDE China
   │     ├── Holds a valid Chinese passport → use it (see 4.2)
   │     └── Otherwise → Travel Document (旅行证) from a Chinese embassy/consulate
   │                     (first issue: usually the post in the country of birth)
   └── INSIDE China
         ├── Holds a valid Travel Document → leave on it (multi-entry within validity)
         ├── Has hukou + a valid Chinese passport → passport; leaving for the other-nationality
         │     country usually still needs an Exit-Entry Permit or a pro forma visa (see 4.5, 7.3)
         └── Otherwise → Exit-Entry Permit (出入境通行证): 3 months, one exit + one entry,
                         from the prefecture-level exit-entry office where you live
```

## 8.3 Key Takeaways

1. **China does not recognize dual nationality** for Chinese citizens. If your child is Chinese under Chinese law, that is the only nationality China recognizes [\[S1\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013967/content.html)[\[S12\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=XnD%2Fs0j%2FZ6YV1zBzlKiEEg%3D%3D).

2. **The parents' status at the time of birth decides.** For a child born abroad with a foreign nationality at birth: if **either** Chinese parent held foreign permanent residency at the birth, the child is not Chinese; if no Chinese parent did, the child is Chinese [\[S6\]](https://gat.zj.gov.cn/art/2015/1/9/art_1229098253_600299.html)[\[S21\]](https://gb.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_137163/lszj/hzlxz/sbhzlxzxz/202312/t20231215_11202867.htm)[\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).

3. **Nationality-conflict persons cannot get Chinese visas.** The consulate decides between visa and Travel Document; parents cannot choose [\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)[\[S33\]](http://wqb.hunan.gov.cn/xxgk/lbxx/201903/t20190320_5298474.html).

4. **The document depends on location and what the person already holds** (see 8.1; trade-offs in 4.5). A person in China with a valid Travel Document simply leaves on it [\[S10\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=NVp1yR02CHoC6IjdpMqppg%3D%3D).

5. **The Exit-Entry Permit is valid for 3 months for one exit and one entry** [\[S5\]](http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2532756.html). Staying abroad longer means applying for a Travel Document there [\[S23\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/lszj/hzlxz/202506/t20250625_11658204.htm).

6. **Turning 18 does not change the status automatically.** Nationality is determined when documents are applied for, and consular practice varies, especially if the family later settles abroad [\[S11\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/Enquiry/publish/showQuestion.jsp?MZ=pkmAunjGDGzbiy1XkMpfaA%3D%3D)[\[S20\]](https://jm.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/hzqz/sqxz/202311/t20231129_11188824.htm)[\[S22\]](https://edinburgh.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/hzlxz/202308/t20230824_11132135.htm).

7. **Hukou and the Exit-Entry Permit can coexist** [\[S36\]](https://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202301/t20230103_6086342.htm)[\[S39\]](http://gdga.gd.gov.cn/bsfw/zsk/qt/content/post_3368370.html), but local *hukou* rules vary (e.g., Fuzhou's declaration on foreign citizenship) [\[S42\]](https://gaj.fuzhou.gov.cn/jwxx/ztlm/hzywbszn/hwbbszn/cssb_62028/202202/t20220211_4307783.htm).

8. **Treaty-based travel facilitation exists only with some countries** (US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand) [\[S25\]](https://nagoya.china-consulate.gov.cn/lsfw/a1/202506/t20250620_11653715.htm)[\[S7\]](https://losangeles.china-consulate.gov.cn/lbqw/gywm/lxlqz/201710/t20171020_5539493.htm). Pro forma visas (US; reportedly France) are discretionary and for minors; the UK's right-of-abode certificate is a published alternative (7.3). Visa-free entry with the foreign passport should not be assumed [\[S3\]](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html)[\[S34\]](https://au.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/qz/202511/t20251112_11751551.htm).

9. **Always check with local authorities.** Rules vary by province, city, consulate and port. Consult the local public security exit-entry office (in China) or the Chinese embassy/consulate for your district (abroad).

## VIII. NIA Enquiry Replies: Original Text

Every National Immigration Administration reply relied on in this guide, as published in the NIA's 咨询建议 system. Quotations are the 回复内容 verbatim.

**[S10] 旅行证有效期问题咨询** — No. 6305262814, asked 5 April 2021, answered 13 April 2021 by 边防检查管理司. The grandfather of a US-born girl with two Chinese parents and no foreign green card asked whether her Travel Document still worked after her 18th birthday, citing the rumour *"18周岁以后旅行证自动失效，必须办理中国签证"*.

> *"《中华人民共和国旅行证》一般为两年有效，期间持证人可多次入出境，备注页或末页上标注有'仅限一次入出境使用'或'仅限回国使用'章的，应按照此标注通行。"*

**[S11] 国籍冲突子女18周岁以后入境时需要办理旅行证入境吗？** — No. 9866320905, asked 22 September 2020, answered 29 September 2020 by 公民出入境管理司. A foreign father and Chinese mother asked which document their newly adult child should use.

> *"根据《国籍法》有关规定，丧失中国国籍有以下两种情形：一、根据《国籍法》第九条规定，'定居外国的中国公民，自愿加入或取得外国国籍的，即自动丧失中国国籍'；二、根据《国籍法》第十一条规定，'申请退出中国国籍获得批准的, 即丧失中国国籍。' 以上供你参考。相关子女向中国主管部门申请办理出入境证件时，有关部门将会根据具体情况对其国籍进行认定。"*

**[S12] 国籍冲突小孩18周岁以后** — No. 6463724017, asked 20 October 2020, answered 27 October 2020 by 公民出入境管理司. A child refused a residence permit because of the nationality conflict, who had been travelling on single-use Exit-Entry Permits, had now turned 18; the parent asked whether he could go on living in China.

> *"根据《国籍法》第三条规定，中华人民共和国公民不承认中国公民具有双重国籍。依法具有中国国籍的人，我国对其持有的外国国籍不予承认。存在国籍冲突的人员出入境，可向当地出入境管理部门申请，由出入境管理部门对当事人国籍进行认定后，依法办理相应出入境证件。"*

(The first sentence is garbled in the original; Article 3 reads 中华人民共和国不承认中国公民具有双重国籍.)

**[S67] 国籍冲突是否丧失中国籍** — No. 4695064085, asked 11 September 2026, answered 24 September 2026 by 公民出入境管理司. A person born in China, foreign by descent, schooled and resident in China throughout on Travel Documents, now adult, asked whether the foreign nationality costs them Chinese nationality and how to stay lawfully.

> *"《中华人民共和国国籍法》第九条规定：定居外国的中国公民，自愿加入或取得外国国籍的，即自动丧失中国国籍。因您未详细说明具体情况，关于是否存在自动丧失中国国籍的问题，建议您向所在地公安机关出入境管理部门咨询。如有其他问题，您也可以拨打移民管理机构12367服务电话咨询。"*

**[S68] 国籍判定和户口问题** — No. 7161963873, asked 8 January 2023, answered 17 January 2023 by 公民出入境管理司. A New Zealand-born child of two Chinese parents who had not settled abroad was issued a Travel Document by the embassy; the question was whether he counts as Chinese despite having no *hukou* and no Resident ID Card, and whether he could enrol at a Chinese university as a foreign student.

> *"《国籍法》第五条规定，父母双方或一方为中国公民，本人出生在外国，具有中国国籍;但父母双方或一方为中国公民并定居在外国，本人出生时即具有外国国籍的，不具有中国国籍。如您有具体业务需求，可向所在地公安机关出入境管理部门咨询。"*

**[S69] 旅行证不足6个月** — No. 2483697632, asked 24 January 2025, answered 8 February 2025 by 边防检查管理司. The holder of a Travel Document issued 17 July 2023 asked whether it could still be used for a trip to China with under 6 months left.

> *"出入境证件在有效期内可正常办理出入境手续。"*

**[S70] 儿子法国护照旅行证回中国，有效期不足6个月，是否影响出入境？** — No. 7476449492, asked 15 July 2025, answered 21 July 2025 by 边防检查管理司. A Chinese mother asked about her 4-year-old son, a French national flying on a French passport and entering China on a Travel Document, both close to expiry.

> *"据您所述情形，可持有效出入境证件及有效签证（免签除外），经边检机关查验准许后正常出入境。只要出入境当日该护照仍在签发有效期内，即为有效出入境证件。"*

**[S71] 异地办理国籍恢复** — No. 7041576636, asked 8 April 2026, answered 17 April 2026 by 公民出入境管理司. A German national with *hukou* in Fujian asked whether restoration had to be applied for there rather than in Zhejiang, where they now live.

> *"您可向居留地、原户籍所在地或者中国籍父母（原）户籍所在地的公安机关出入境管理部门提出恢复中国国籍申请。如有其他问题，您也可拨打移民管理机构12367服务电话咨询。"*
