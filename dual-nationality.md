# A Guide for Parents of Children with Chinese and Other Nationalities
## Navigating Nationality Conflicts, Identity Documents, and Travel

---

# Introduction

This guide is designed for parents of children who hold—or may hold—both Chinese nationality and one or more other nationalities. The term **国籍冲突 (nationality conflict)** refers to a situation where a child is considered a Chinese national under Chinese law but also holds a foreign nationality under the law of another country.

Because the **《中华人民共和国国籍法》 (Nationality Law of the People's Republic of China)** does not recognize dual nationality, families face unique challenges when it comes to birth registration, identity documents, and international travel. This guide walks you through the process from birth to adulthood, explaining the rules, requirements, and practical considerations at each stage.

---

# Part One: Understanding the Basics

## 1.1 Key Terms and Definitions

| **English Term** | **Chinese Official Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|---|
| Nationality | 国籍 (*Guójí*) | The legal bond between an individual and a state. China does not recognize dual nationality. |
| Citizenship | 公民身份 (*Gōngmín Shēnfèn*) | Under Chinese law, synonymous with nationality—all Chinese nationals are Chinese citizens. |
| Household Registration | 户口 (*Hùkǒu*) | The family register that records a person's official place of residence in China. |
| Permanent Residency (abroad) | 定居 (*Dìngjū*) / 永久居留 (*Yǒngjiǔ Jūliú*) | The right to reside permanently in a foreign country. Under Chinese law, this is a key factor in determining whether a person has lost Chinese nationality. |
| Passport | 护照 (*Hùzhào*) | A document issued to Chinese citizens for international travel, proving nationality and identity abroad. |
| Travel Document | 旅行证 (*Lǚxíngzhèng*) | A passport substitute issued to Chinese citizens who are inconvenienced or not required to hold a passport—including persons with nationality conflicts. |
| Exit-Entry Permit | 出入境通行证 (*Chūrùjìng Tōngxíngzhèng*) | A通行证件 for entering and exiting China, typically issued as a 3-month, single-entry document for nationality conflict cases. |
| Visa | 签证 (*Qiānzhèng*) | A conditional authorization granted by a country to a foreigner for entry. **Persons with nationality conflicts cannot be issued Chinese visas in their foreign passports**. |
| Residence Permit | 居留许可 (*Jūliú Xǔkě*) | A document for foreigners staying in China for more than 180 days. |

## 1.2 The Core Legal Principle

> *“中华人民共和国不承认中国公民具有双重国籍。”* (The People's Republic of China does not recognize dual nationality for any Chinese citizen.)

When a person has both Chinese nationality and foreign nationality, **China recognizes only the Chinese nationality** and does not recognize the foreign nationality. Therefore, Chinese embassies and consulates **cannot issue a Chinese visa** in the person's foreign passport—instead, they must issue a Chinese Travel Document or Exit-Entry Permit.

## 1.3 How Nationality Is Determined Under Chinese Law

The **《中华人民共和国国籍法》 (Nationality Law of the PRC)** sets out the following rules:

- **Article 4**: A person born in China whose parents are both Chinese nationals or one of whom is a Chinese national shall have Chinese nationality.
- **Article 5**: A person born abroad whose parents are both Chinese nationals or one of whom is a Chinese national shall have Chinese nationality—**except** where the parents (or the Chinese-national parent) have **settled abroad** and the child acquired foreign nationality at birth.
- **Article 9**: Chinese citizens who **settle abroad** and **voluntarily acquire foreign nationality** automatically lose Chinese nationality.
- **Article 3**: The PRC does not recognize dual nationality.

**For parents, this means:**

| **Scenario** | **Child's Chinese Nationality Status** |
|---|---|
| Child born in China to one Chinese parent | ✅ Has Chinese nationality |
| Child born abroad to Chinese parent(s) who have **not** settled abroad (no permanent residency) | ✅ Has Chinese nationality (nationality conflict likely) |
| Child born abroad to Chinese parent(s) who **have settled abroad** (have permanent residency) and child acquires foreign nationality at birth | ❌ Does **not** have Chinese nationality |
| Chinese citizen settles abroad and voluntarily acquires foreign nationality | ❌ Automatically loses Chinese nationality |

# Part Two: Birth and Early Years

## 2.1 Before Birth: What to Consider

If you are a Chinese citizen living abroad or in a跨国 marriage (cross-national marriage), consider:

1. **Where will the child be born?** The place of birth affects whether the child acquires foreign nationality at birth.
2. **Do you have permanent residency abroad?** This determines whether your child retains Chinese nationality under Article 5 of the Nationality Law.
3. **Where will the child primarily reside?** This affects which travel documents will be needed.

## 2.2 At Birth: Nationality Determination

**For children born abroad:**

> *“父母双方或一方为中国公民，本人出生在外国，具有中国国籍；但父母双方或一方为中国公民并定居在外国，本人出生时即具有外国国籍的，不具有中国国籍。”*

**Translation**: A child born abroad to one or two Chinese-national parents has Chinese nationality—**unless** the Chinese-national parent(s) had settled abroad (held permanent residency) at the time of birth, in which case the child does **not** have Chinese nationality.

**For children born in China to one foreign parent:**

> *“在中国出生，父母一方为中国公民，另一方为外国公民，且按照外籍父母国籍国法律，出生即具有外国国籍的儿童”*

Such children have Chinese nationality under Chinese law but may also have foreign nationality—creating a **nationality conflict**.

## 2.3 The Birth Certificate – Name Requirements

### If the child is born in China

The **《出生医学证明》 (Medical Certificate of Birth)** must use **standard Chinese characters** from the **《通用规范汉字表》 (Universal Standard Chinese Character Table)** .

**Exception**: If one parent is foreign, the child's name may be filled in **either Chinese or a foreign language**. However, if the parents plan to register *hukou* in China, the name **must** be in standard Chinese characters. A birth certificate with a foreign name is **defective for hukou purposes** and must be replaced before registration.

### If the child is born abroad

The foreign birth certificate will typically show the name in the local language. For Chinese purposes:

- When applying for a Travel Document or Exit-Entry Permit, you will register the child's **Chinese name** with its standard Hanyu Pinyin.
- The foreign name from the birth certificate may be added as an **endorsement (加注)** on the observation page of the passport or Travel Document.
- For *hukou* registration in China, the birth certificate must be translated into Chinese by a qualified translation company.

### Name Change After Registration

If you wish to change the child's name after *hukou* registration:

- **For minors under 18**: Parents or legal guardians apply jointly. If the child is **8 years or older**, the child's own consent is required.
- **For adults (18+)**: Apply in person.

# Part Three: Household Registration (Hukou)

## 3.1 What Is Hukou and Why Does It Matter?

**户口 (*Hukou*)** is the official family register in China. It records a person's identity, place of residence, and family relationships. Having *hukou* is important for access to education, healthcare, and many other services in China.

**Important**: For persons with nationality conflicts, *hukou* registration does **not** mean the person has lost Chinese nationality. Conversely, not having *hukou* does not mean the person lacks Chinese nationality.

## 3.2 Registering Hukou for a Child Born Abroad

**Eligibility**: A child born abroad may apply for *hukou* registration in China if the child has Chinese nationality and at least one parent has Chinese *hukou* in the place of registration.

**Required materials** (general):
- Foreign birth certificate (original + copy)
- Certified Chinese translation by a qualified translation company
- Parent's Resident Household Register (*户口簿*) and Resident ID Card
- Parents' marriage certificate (if applicable)
- Child's Chinese Travel Document or Exit-Entry Permit used for entry into China
- If the child holds a foreign passport: nationality determination certificate from the exit-entry administration

**If the child is 6 years or older**:

> *“年满6周岁不满18周岁的，还应当提交亲子鉴定证明，逐级报公安分局审核后办理。”*

**Translation**: For children aged 6 to under 18, a **亲子鉴定证明 (paternity testing certificate / DNA test)** must be submitted, and the application must be reviewed level-by-level up to the Public Security Sub-bureau.

Additional requirements for children **6 years or older**:
- Community police officer investigation and interview
- Photo, fingerprint, and DNA comparison through the national anti-trafficking database
- Approval at the county or higher level

**If the child is under 6 years old**: The application is generally processed at the police station level and can be completed more quickly.

## 3.3 Nationality Determination for Hukou Registration

If the child's nationality status is unclear (e.g., the child holds a foreign passport), the public security bureau may require:

> *“持外国护照入境或申请人国籍情况存有疑义的，经设区市（含）以上公安机关出入境管理部门认定具有中国国籍的”*

**Translation**: If the child entered with a foreign passport or the nationality status is in doubt, the exit-entry administration at the prefecture level or above must first **determine that the child has Chinese nationality** before *hukou* registration can proceed.

For children born in China with nationality conflicts, no separate nationality determination is required—they follow the standard domestic birth registration process.

## 3.4 What If the Child Already Has Hukou?

If the child already has *hukou* in China, this does **not** affect their nationality conflict status. The child may still need to use Travel Documents or Exit-Entry Permits for international travel.

# Part Four: Identity Documents

## 4.1 Overview: Which Document for Which Situation?

| **Document** | **Issuing Authority** | **Typical Validity** | **When to Use** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chinese Passport** (普通护照) | Public security exit-entry authorities (domestic) | 5-10 years | Chinese citizens **without** nationality conflicts |
| **Travel Document** (旅行证) | Chinese embassies/consulates (overseas) | 2 years, multiple entries | **Nationality conflict persons located outside China** |
| **Exit-Entry Permit** (出入境通行证) | Public security exit-entry authorities (domestic) | 3 months, single entry | **Nationality conflict persons located inside China** |

## 4.2 Can a Person with Nationality Conflict Get a Chinese Passport?

**Generally, no.** The **《中华人民共和国护照法》 (Passport Law of the PRC)** states that passports shall not be issued to persons who:

> *“不具有中华人民共和国国籍的”* (Do not have Chinese nationality)

While a person with nationality conflict does have Chinese nationality, in practice, exit-entry authorities often **refuse to issue a Chinese passport** to a person who also holds a foreign passport, advising them to use an Exit-Entry Permit or Travel Document instead.

**Exception**: Some families have successfully obtained Chinese passports for nationality-conflict children by using **pro forma visas** (形式签证) from the foreign country's consulate—but this is discretionary and not guaranteed (see Part Seven).

## 4.3 Applying for a Travel Document (Overseas)

**Who qualifies?**

> *“事实上存在国籍冲突的人员”* (Persons with de facto nationality conflicts)

**Specific categories**:
1. Children born abroad who acquired foreign nationality at birth, with at least one Chinese-national parent who **had not** obtained foreign permanent residency at the time of birth
2. Children born in China to one Chinese and one foreign parent, who acquired foreign nationality at birth under the foreign parent's nationality law

**Application process**:
- Submit application through the **“中国领事”APP (China Consular Affairs APP)**
- Upload photos and required documents
- Track progress through the APP
- Appear in person at the embassy/consulate when required

**Required materials**:
- 6-month recent photo (white background)
- Parents' valid passports (information pages)
- Parents' foreign residence permits or visas at the time of the child's birth
- Child's birth certificate
- Child's foreign passport (if available)
- 《同意为未成年人办理护照/旅行证声明书》 (Consent Declaration for Minor's Passport/Travel Document) signed by at least one parent
- Proof of guardianship (birth certificate, *hukou*, etc.)
- Exit-Entry Permit or *hukou* / Resident ID Card (if available)

**For minors under 16**: At least one parent or legal guardian must accompany the child and provide their identity documents.

**Renewal (换发)** : Required when the Travel Document has less than 6 months of validity remaining.

## 4.4 Applying for an Exit-Entry Permit (Domestic)

**Who qualifies?**

> *“申请人因国籍冲突，不便持用普通护照的”* (Applicants who, due to nationality conflicts, are inconvenienced to hold ordinary passports)

**Standard issuance**: 3-month, single-entry Exit-Entry Permit

**Required materials**:
- Completed 《中华人民共和国出入境通行证申请表》 (PRC Exit-Entry Permit Application Form) with photo
- Birth certificate
- Foreign passport held by the applicant
- Parents' identity documents and proof of local residence (e.g., Guangdong Residence Permit)
- If entered using a Travel Document: the Travel Document
- Latest entry stamp copy
- If renewing: original Exit-Entry Permit
- If born abroad to Chinese parent(s): parents' Chinese passports and foreign visa/residence permit held at time of birth
- Proof of travel to the country of nationality conflict (e.g., flight tickets)
- If *hukou* is registered: Resident ID Card and Household Register
- Marriage certificate (if applicable)

**For minors under 18**: May be handled by a guardian.

**For minors under 16**: Must be accompanied by a guardian, who must submit guardianship proof (birth certificate, *hukou*, notarized guardianship document).

**Processing time**: 5 working days (regular); 3 working days (emergency)

**Fee**: RMB 15

# Part Five: Travel – Scenarios and Requirements

## 5.1 Core Principle for Border Crossing

Chinese border inspection requires that a Chinese citizen exiting China must present **proof of legal permission to enter the destination country**. For persons with nationality conflicts, this is where the Travel Document or Exit-Entry Permit comes in.

**Key rule**: If you are determined to be a Chinese national, you **must** use Chinese travel documents (Passport, Travel Document, or Exit-Entry Permit) to enter and exit China. You **cannot** use a foreign passport with a Chinese visa.

## 5.2 Scenario 1: Child Resides Outside China, Traveling to China

**Document needed**: **Travel Document** (旅行证)

- Validity: 2 years, multiple entries
- Apply at the local Chinese embassy or consulate
- The child enters China as a Chinese citizen—no visa required

**If the Travel Document expires while in China**:
- Apply for a single-use Exit-Entry Permit from the local public security exit-entry administration
- Use this permit to exit China

## 5.3 Scenario 2: Child Resides in China, Traveling Abroad

**Document needed**: **Exit-Entry Permit** (出入境通行证)

- Validity: 3 months, single entry
- Apply at the local public security exit-entry administration
- Each trip abroad requires a new application

**For frequent travelers**: There is no standard provision for multi-entry Exit-Entry Permits for nationality conflict cases. Each trip requires a new 3-month single-entry permit.

**For extended stays abroad (>90 days)** : The Exit-Entry Permit is single-use—once you exit China, it is spent. For the return journey, you must apply for a Travel Document at the local Chinese embassy or consulate abroad.

## 5.4 Scenario 3: Travel Document Expired While in China

If the Travel Document expires while the child is in China:

> *“若您的孩子是持我驻外使领馆签发的旅行证回国，那您的孩子应是具有中国国籍，若其因国籍冲突，不便持用普通护照，可前往当地公安机关出入境管理部门申请三个月一次出境有效的出入境通行证。”*

**Translation**: If your child entered China with a Travel Document issued by a Chinese embassy/consulate, the child has Chinese nationality. If the child is inconvenienced to hold an ordinary passport due to nationality conflict, you may apply for a 3-month single-exit Exit-Entry Permit from the local public security exit-entry administration.

## 5.5 Scenario 4: What About Using a Foreign Passport + Chinese Visa?

**This will not work.** Chinese embassies and consulates **cannot** issue Chinese visas in the foreign passports of persons with nationality conflicts. If a person with nationality conflict attempts to enter China using a foreign passport with a Chinese visa, border inspection will **block entry** and may initiate nationality determination proceedings.

## 5.6 Scenario 5: Using a Chinese Passport with a "Pro Forma Visa"

Some countries (e.g., the United States, France) may issue a **pro forma visa** (形式签证) in the Chinese passport of a dual-national child, allowing the child to exit China using only the Chinese passport.

**Key points**:
- This is a **discretionary courtesy**, not a legal right
- Generally available **only for minors**; adults are typically not eligible
- Not officially recognized in Chinese law
- Acceptance at border inspection depends on the specific officer and port
- Does **not** change the child's nationality conflict status

**Risk**: If border inspection discovers the child holds both a Chinese passport and a foreign passport, the child may be **stopped** and required to complete nationality determination procedures.

# Part Six: Adulthood (Age 18 and Above)

## 6.1 Does Nationality Conflict Automatically End at 18?

**No.** The Nationality Conflict status does **not** automatically disappear upon reaching age 18.

The **National Immigration Administration (国家移民管理局)** has confirmed:

> *“相关子女向中国主管部门申请办理出入境证件时，有关部门将会根据具体情况对其国籍进行认定。”*

**Translation**: When such children apply to Chinese authorities for exit-entry documents, the relevant departments will determine their nationality based on the specific circumstances.

**Two ways to lose Chinese nationality**:
1. **Article 9 of the Nationality Law**: Settle abroad and voluntarily acquire foreign nationality → automatic loss
2. **Article 11 of the Nationality Law**: Apply to renounce Chinese nationality and receive approval

If **neither** applies, the person **retains Chinese nationality** and **must continue using Travel Documents or Exit-Entry Permits**—they cannot switch to a foreign passport with a Chinese visa.

## 6.2 Can an Adult Apply for a Travel Document?

**Yes.** Chinese embassies explicitly include **adults** in their Travel Document provisions. The Chinese Consulate in Los Angeles states:

> *“申请人为未满16周岁的未成年人 ... 或首次办理的成年人”* (Applicants who are minors under 16 ... or adults applying for the first time)

**For adults applying for a Travel Document**:
- Must apply in person
- Facial recognition required in the APP
- Must upload entry-exit records from the immigration mini-program
- No guardian accompaniment required

## 6.3 Can an Adult Apply for an Exit-Entry Permit?

**Yes.** The regulations for Exit-Entry Permits do **not** have an age limit. Adults with nationality conflicts may apply for the same 3-month single-entry Exit-Entry Permit.

**Application for adults**: Apply in person with valid ID—no guardian required.

## 6.4 What If the Adult Has Hukou?

Having *hukou* does **not** change the document requirements. The adult must still use Travel Documents or Exit-Entry Permits for international travel if they retain Chinese nationality.

## 6.5 Can an Adult Renounce Chinese Nationality?

**Yes**, under certain conditions.

**《国籍法》第十条 (Nationality Law Article 10)** : A Chinese citizen may apply to renounce Chinese nationality if they meet **one** of the following conditions:
1. A close relative who is a foreign national
2. Settled abroad
3. Other legitimate reasons

**Who cannot renounce**:

> *“国家工作人员和现役军人，不得退出中国国籍。”* (State functionaries and active-duty military personnel shall not renounce Chinese nationality.)

**For minors**: Applications to renounce Chinese nationality must be handled by parents or legal guardians.

# Part Seven: Special Considerations

## 7.1 Hukou and Exit-Entry Permits – No Conflict

> *“我国公民因国籍冲突不便于持用普通护照时，可以申请办理中华人民共和国出入境通行证，与是否需注销户籍无关。”*

**Translation**: Chinese citizens who are inconvenienced to hold ordinary passports due to nationality conflicts may apply for a PRC Exit-Entry Permit, which is **unrelated to whether hukou needs to be cancelled**.

Having *hukou* does **not** prevent you from applying for an Exit-Entry Permit. Conversely, applying for an Exit-Entry Permit does **not** require hukou cancellation.

## 7.2 Pro Forma Visas – A Practical Workaround

Some families with U.S.-China or France-China dual-national children have successfully used **pro forma visas** (形式签证) placed in the child's Chinese passport.

**How it works**:
1. The child obtains a Chinese passport
2. The U.S. or French consulate places a "pro forma visa" stamp in the Chinese passport
3. At Chinese border inspection (exiting China): the child presents only the Chinese passport with the pro forma visa
4. At the U.S. or French border: the child presents the foreign passport

**Important limitations**:
- Generally available **only for minors**; adults are typically not eligible
- It is a **discretionary courtesy**, not a legal right
- Acceptance at Chinese border inspection is not guaranteed
- If the child has **lost** Chinese nationality under Article 9, using a Chinese passport is **illegal** regardless of any visa stamps

## 7.3 What About DNA Testing for Hukou Registration?

For children **6 years or older** applying for *hukou* registration:

> *“年满6周岁不满18周岁的，还应当提交亲子鉴定证明”*

**Translation**: For those aged 6 to under 18, a **亲子鉴定证明 (paternity testing certificate / DNA test)** must be submitted.

**Additional requirements for children 6+**:
- Community police officer investigation
- Photo, fingerprint, and DNA comparison through the national anti-trafficking database
- Level-by-level approval up to the Public Security Sub-bureau

**DNA testing institutions**: Must be registered in the **国家司法鉴定名录网 (National Judicial Authentication Directory Network)** .

# Part Eight: Summary and Quick Reference

## 8.1 Document Decision Tree
```

Is the person located outside China?
├── YES → Apply for Travel Document (旅行证) at Chinese embassy/consulate
└── NO (in China) → Apply for Exit-Entry Permit (出入境通行证) at local public security bureau

```
## 8.2 Age-Based Summary

| **Age Group** | **Travel Document Application** | **Exit-Entry Permit Application** | **Hukou Registration** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 6 | Guardian accompaniment required | Guardian accompaniment required | Police station level; immediate processing |
| 6 to under 16 | Guardian accompaniment & consent required | Guardian accompaniment required | DNA test required; level-by-level approval |
| 16 to under 18 | Guardian accompaniment & consent required | May be handled by guardian | DNA test required; level-by-level approval |
| 18+ (Adult) | Independent application; facial recognition | Independent application | Personal application; may require DNA if restoring cancelled hukou |

## 8.3 Key Takeaways

1. **China does not recognize dual nationality.** If your child has Chinese nationality under Chinese law, that is the only nationality China recognizes.

2. **Persons with nationality conflicts cannot get Chinese visas.** They must use Travel Documents or Exit-Entry Permits.

3. **The document depends on location.** Outside China → Travel Document. Inside China → Exit-Entry Permit.

4. **Age does not automatically change the status.** At 18, the authorities re-examine nationality. If the person still has Chinese nationality, they must continue using Travel Documents or Exit-Entry Permits.

5. **Hukou does not affect travel document eligibility.** Having *hukou* does not prevent applying for an Exit-Entry Permit, and applying for an Exit-Entry Permit does not require hukou cancellation.

6. **Pro forma visas are discretionary.** They are not a legal right and are generally only available for minors.

7. **Always check with local authorities.** Rules may vary by province, city, and port. Consult the local public security exit-entry administration (in China) or the nearest Chinese embassy/consulate (abroad) for the most current requirements.
